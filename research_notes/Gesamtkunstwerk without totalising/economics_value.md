# Economics of Hype vs. Timeless Craft: Why Some Designed Objects Hold Value and Others Crash

Research notes compiled 27 Oct 2026. Every figure below has an inline source. Secondary or promotional sources are flagged. Computed figures (percentages derived from cited numbers) are marked "calc." in the Inferences sections.

## 1. Hype/drop culture: Supreme, sneaker resale, Nike's pullback

### Takeaway
The hype economy lost value fast. VF Corp paid about $2.1bn for Supreme in 2020 and sold it for $1.5bn in 2024, a nominal loss of about 29%. The sneaker resale market cooled in 2022–2024 as Nike flooded and then cut supply of its hype franchises (Dunk, AJ1, AF1). Value that rests on manufactured scarcity disappears when supply or attention changes.

### Cited Findings
- **Supreme (verified):** EssilorLuxottica agreed on 17 July 2024 to buy Supreme from VF Corp for **$1.5bn in cash**. VF had bought it in 2020 for **about $2.1bn**. Supreme had **$538m revenue** in VF's latest fiscal year. VF said a strategic review found "limited synergies" and that the sale would be dilutive to EPS in FY2025. — [VF Corp press release](https://vfc.com/investors/news-events-presentations/press-releases/detail/1839/essilorluxottica-to-acquire-supreme-from-vf-corporation); [VF SEC 8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/103379/000119312524179856/d871255dex991.htm); [Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/vf-sells-supreme-essilorluxottica/721639/)
- **StockX valuation and layoffs:** StockX raised money in early 2021 at a **$3.8bn valuation** ("nearly $4bn" in another report). It cut about **8% of staff in June 2022**, citing "macroeconomic challenges", cut about **80 corporate roles in Nov 2022**, and restructured about **40 roles in Jan 2024**, including the CMO's departure. One analysis found that **average prices rose 11% while US unit sales fell 12%**. — [Retail Dive (8% layoff)](https://www.retaildive.com/news/stockx-lays-off-8-percent-workforce/626280/); [Retail Dive (Nov 2022)](https://www.retaildive.com/news/stockx-downsizes-yeezy/635691/); [Modern Retail, Jan 2024](https://www.modernretail.co/operations/stockx-confirms-layoffs-and-cmo-departure/)
- **StockX "State of Resale" 2024 (first 7 months of 2024):** Nike's resale market share fell **11%** and Jordan's fell **12%** year on year. **Nike Dunk trades fell 41%** year on year. StockX said resale "isn't dead" but is "diversifying" (for example, Asics resale reportedly **+589%** in 2024). This is StockX's own data, so the company has an interest in the "not dead" framing. — [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/fashion/stockx-report-sneaker-resale-isnt-dead/2024082377226); [SGI Europe](https://www.sgieurope.com/retail/stockx-resale-report-asics-soars-589-in-2024-as-sneaker-resale-market-evolves/110266.article); [Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/stockx-co-founder-named-ceo-sneaker-resale-declines/732373)
- **Air Jordan 1 fell about 18% in 2024** (StockX data as analysed by an investment blog; low reliability). — [Angel Investors Network](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/alternative-investments/sneaker-resale-alternative-asset-data)
- **Nike pullback:** Under CEO Elliott Hill, Nike deliberately shrank Air Force 1, Air Jordan 1 and Dunk. In Q3 FY2025, revenue fell **9% to $11.3bn**, and the CFO said the three franchises would fall by about **10 percentage points of footwear mix** by Q4. Hill said they were not being discontinued. — [Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/nike-rightsizing-air-force-air-jordan-dunk-styles-sales-decline/743179/); [Fashion Dive](https://www.fashiondive.com/news/nike-rightsizing-air-force-air-jordan-dunk-styles-sales-decline/743286/)
- **Size of the Dunk business:** Piper Sandler estimated Dunk sales at about **$5.85bn in FY2024 (about 18% of Nike footwear)**, with analysts expecting a fall to about **$1.75bn**. This is an analyst estimate. — [Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/negocios/el-zapato-mas-popular-de-nike-esta-desapareciendo-la-franquicia-batalla-contra-la-crisis/)
- Commentary links the fall in resale values to Nike raising production of Dunk and AJ1 during the boom. This is interpretation, not a measured finding. — [PowerCommerce](https://powercommerce.com/blogs/ecommerce-hub/the-sneaker-resale-market-from-boom-to-bust-and-beyond)

### Inferences
- Supreme's nominal write-down was ($2.1bn − $1.5bn) / $2.1bn ≈ **−29%** in four years (calc.). Allowing for 2020–2024 inflation, the real loss is larger. A brand whose value rested on drops and queues lost almost a third of its acquisition value inside one corporate holding period.
- Nike shows the paradox of hype: the brand created scarcity, then monetised the hype by expanding supply, which destroyed the resale premium, and then had to cut supply. A hype object's value depends on the issuer's supply decisions and not on the object.
- The figure "prices +11%, units −12%" fits a market in which a smaller group of buyers paid more for fewer pairs, which is typical of a speculative top.

### Gaps
- No reliable 2025–2026 StockX or GOAT price index or valuation was found. GOAT's latest valuation ($3.7bn in 2021, from memory) was not verified this session.
- No StockX price index covering the whole 2021–2024 correction was found. Most published data counts trades or market share, not prices.

## 2. Luxury slowdown 2024–2026, logo fatigue and price hikes

### Takeaway
After a post-Covid boom driven mostly by price increases, luxury contracted. Bain-Altagamma estimates 2025 spending of about €1.44tn, down 1–3% at current exchange rates. The customer base fell from about 400m (2022) to about 330–340m (2025). Kering/Gucci, the leading logo house of the 2015–2022 era, fell about 19–22% in 2025. Chanel's Classic Flap more than doubled in price between 2016 and 2025. Hermès, which grows through craft capacity rather than logos, grew 9%.

### Cited Findings
- **Bain-Altagamma, Dec 2025:** global luxury spending was about **€1.44tn in 2025**, a change of **−1% to −3% at current FX** and **−1% to +1% at constant FX**, still **12–14% above 2019**. Bain describes this as "normalisation" after the post-Covid boom. — [Bain, "Finding a new longevity for luxury"](https://www.bain.com/insights/finding-a-new-longevity-for-luxury/) (figures as summarised in search; the page could not be fetched directly this session)
- **Customers lost:** Bain's 2024 study found the luxury customer base shrank by **about 50m in two years from about 400m**. Bain's Federica Levato attributed this partly to brands moving products into higher price bands, which alienated younger and Gen Z buyers. — [The Drinks Business, Nov 2024](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2024/11/luxury-market-loses-50-million-customers/); [Inside Retail](https://insideretail.com.au/business/financial/bain-says-2024-one-of-the-weakest-years-on-record-for-luxury-sales-202411)
- **A further ~20m customers lost in 2025**, leaving an active base of about **330m**. Another report gives **340m, of whom 40–45% are active buyers**. The two figures conflict and come from secondary sources. — [RetailBoss](https://retailboss.co/luxury-faces-first-real-decline-in-15-years-as-50-million-consumers-exit/); [Bluewin](https://www.bluewin.ch/en/news/the-rich-now-only-spend-1400000000000-francs-on-luxury-2977225.html)
- **Prices:** Bain found **like-for-like prices of iconic bags rose 50–70% from 2019 to 2025**. McKinsey reportedly found that **more than 80% of luxury growth since 2019 came from price increases**. Both are secondary citations. — [RetailBoss / Bain coverage](https://retailboss.co/luxury-faces-first-real-decline-in-15-years-as-50-million-consumers-exit/); [DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/luxury-brands-lose-350-billion-market-value)
- **Chanel Classic Flap (Medium):** **$4,900 in 2016 → $11,300 after the Aug 2025 hike**. That hike was about 4–5% (about $500), from $10,800. Sotheby's notes that Chanel has raised bag prices at least once a year since 2016. — [Sotheby's](https://www.sothebys.com/en/articles/understanding-the-latest-2025-chanel-bag-price-hikes-and-the-resale-market)
- **The 2010 baseline was $2,850**, a rise of about 297% to $11,300. A blog source says this is about 7× US CPI inflation over the period. — [Mynt Condition (Substack)](https://myntcondition.substack.com/p/this-bags-value-is-rising-faster)
- **2026 price:** one reseller reports a 2026 rise of about 2–4% to **$11,700**. This is unconfirmed and conflicts with other figures. — [Madison Avenue Couture](https://madisonavenuecouture.com/blogs/news/chanel-price-increases-over-the-last-10-years-and-what-it-means-for-resale-value)
- **Chanel resale:** good-condition Medium Flaps list at about **87–105% of current retail** on The RealReal. — [Mynt Condition](https://myntcondition.substack.com/p/this-bags-value-is-rising-faster)
- **Kering FY2025:** revenue **€14.7bn, −13% reported and −10% comparable**. Recurring operating income **€1.6bn (−33%)**, margin **11.1% vs 14.5%**. Net income from continuing operations was **−€29m**. Q4 was −3% comparable. — [Kering 2025 results via Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/kering-2025-results-sequential-improvement-unlocking-next-phase-sustainable)
- **Gucci FY2025:** about **€6.0bn, −19% comparable** (wholesale −34%). Luxury Tribune reports **−22%**. The difference is probably reported vs comparable, but this was not verified against the segment table. — [Luxury Tribune](https://www.luxurytribune.com/en/kering-still-penalized-by-gucci-in-2025); [World Footwear](https://www.worldfootwear.com/news/kering-posts-third-straight-year-of-falling-sales/11250.html)
- **LVMH FY2025:** group revenue **€80.8bn, −1% organic (−5% reported)**, net profit **€10.9bn (−13%)**. Fashion & Leather Goods: **€37.8bn, −8% reported and −5% organic**, profit from recurring operations −13%, margin about 35%. Q4 F&LG was −3% organic. Arnault said conditions would stay difficult in 2026. — [World Footwear](https://www.worldfootwear.com/news/lvmh-posts-resilient-performance-in-2025/11212.html); [Retail Insight Network](https://www.retail-insight-network.com/news/lvmh-2025-revenue-slips/); [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/lvmh-desirability-sales-slip/)
- **Hermès FY2025 (contrast case):** revenue **about €16bn, +9% constant FX (+5.5% reported)**. Leather goods **+13%**, which the company attributes to "desirability" **and increased production capacity**. Its 24th leather workshop opened in Sept 2025 at L'Isle-d'Espagnac. Recurring operating margin **41%**. — [Hermès 2025 Full-Year Results (MFN)](https://mfn.se/all/a/hermes/hermes-international-2025-full-year-results-a01f2a5e); [World Footwear](https://www.worldfootwear.com/news/hermes-exceeds-16-billion-euros-in-full-year-revenue/11263.html)
- **McKinsey/BoF State of Fashion 2026:** about **71% of executives plan price rises**. High prices are "a significant hurdle for aspirational customers", who are cutting purchase frequency, trading down or moving to resale. Luxury value is now polarised and driven by top spenders. — [Glossy](https://www.glossy.co/fashion/as-tariffs-hit-brands-and-customers-alike-more-price-hikes-are-coming-in-2026/); [FashionNetwork](https://uk.fashionnetwork.com/news/Fashion-executives-are-projecting-more-price-increases-next-year,1783652.html); [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/5-key-trends-shaping-luxury-fashion-sector-in-2026/2026031886952)

### Inferences
- From $4,900 to $11,300 is **+131% in 9 years** (calc.), roughly 9.7% a year compounded. Over the same period US CPI rose very roughly 30–35% (estimate from general knowledge, not verified this session). The bag's "appreciation" is mostly the issuer raising its own list price. Resale at 87–105% of the new retail price means owners gain only because Chanel keeps raising the price of new bags, not because the object has gained anything through use.
- Growth driven by price rather than volume (more than 80% from price, according to McKinsey) priced out about 50–70m aspirational buyers. The logo strategy undermined itself: when a logo is everywhere it no longer signals distinction, and when prices are raised to restore scarcity, buyers leave.
- Gucci (−19 to −22%) versus Hermès (+13% in leather goods) is the clearest contrast in the data for the student's argument. However, Hermès's scarcity is also managed (see §4), so the contrast is "craft-capacity-limited supply vs logo-driven volume" and not "honest vs dishonest".

### Gaps
- The Bain primary page could not be fetched (DNS error), so the figures come from search summaries and secondary coverage.
- The exact Gucci 2025 decline, and whether it is reported or comparable, is not confirmed against Kering's segment table.
- No primary BoF or McKinsey page on "luxury fatigue" was retrieved. The term is used in trade press, but this session found no single definitive source for it.

## 3. Quiet luxury as a market trend, and the critique of it

### Takeaway
"Quiet luxury" brands outperformed. Brunello Cucinelli posted record 2025 revenue of €1.41bn (+11.5% constant FX) while the sector shrank. But quiet luxury is itself a status code readable only by insiders (Bourdieu and Currid-Halkett in practice), and the 2025 Loro Piana labour case shows that "craft" stories can hide exploitative supply chains.

### Cited Findings
- **Brunello Cucinelli FY2025:** revenue **€1.41bn, +11.5% constant FX and +10.1% current FX**, a record. Operating result about **€236m (+11.4%)**, net profit **€142.0m (+10.5%)**. Retail **€947.1m (+12.9%)**, wholesale **€460.7m (+8.5%)**. Guidance is about **+10% for 2026** and about **€1.8bn by 2028**. — [FashionUnited, Jan 2026](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/cucinelli-record-2025-revenue-of-1-41-billion-euros-up-11-5-percent/2026011285670); [Il Sole 24 Ore](https://en.ilsole24ore.com/art/brunello-cucinelli-2025-double-digit-increases-in-revenues-and-profitability-AIwtyqUB)
- **Loro Piana (LVMH):** on **14 July 2025** a Milan court placed it under **judicial administration for one year**. The court found it had "through negligence" facilitated labour exploitation: production was subcontracted to two front firms with no manufacturing capacity, which passed work to Chinese-owned workshops in Italy that used irregular workers without proper wages, hours or safety. Loro Piana said it cut ties within 24 hours of discovering the subcontractors (20 May). The administration was **lifted early in 2026**, and the company cites "2,400 audits" since 2024. — [The Fashion Law](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/loro-piana-faces-court-appointed-oversight-amid-labor-exploitation-allegations/); [Jing Daily](https://jingdaily.com/intels/2025-07/16/milan-court-places-loro-piana-under-administration-over-labor-abuse); [FashionUnited, Apr 2026](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/exploited-labour-loro-piana-lvmh-is-no-longer-under-judicial-administration/2026042187572)
- **Inconspicuous consumption:** Currid-Halkett's *The Sum of Small Things* (Princeton UP, 2017) argues that the "aspirational class" has moved from Veblenian conspicuous goods to "inconspicuous consumption": expensive but subtle goods and investments in human capital (education, health, organic food). Material goods lose symbolic power as they become widely accessible. — [Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/9780691162737); [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/blogs/technology-and-learning/sum-small-things-inconspicuous-consumption-and-small-college-town)
- **Critique:** Danny Dorling (THE review) argues that this so-called inconspicuous consumption "is hardly a small thing" and that the book shows "a great deal of sympathy for the rich". — [Times Higher Education](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/616740)

### Inferences
- Quiet luxury moves the logo from the surface into knowledge: recognising Loro Piana cashmere or a The Row cut requires cultural capital (Bourdieu). It is still distinction, only harder to read. A design student should not claim that "no logo" means "no status game".
- Cucinelli's double-digit growth during a sector decline shows that the market pays for a craft story. That gives the story commercial value, which in turn creates an incentive to fake it (Loro Piana). The honest version of the argument is about verifiable production (traceable makers, repair services, published supply chains), not about an aesthetic.

### Gaps
- The Row's revenue is not public. Reports of a 2024 minority investment at about a $1bn valuation exist but were not verified this session.
- Loro Piana's own revenue is not disclosed separately within LVMH.
- No Bain or McKinsey quote explicitly calling quiet luxury "a trend" was retrieved.

## 4. Objects that appreciate: Birkin, watches, collectible design, Leica, De Sede, Vitsoe

### Takeaway
Appreciation clusters in objects with authorship, provenance and limited craft output. Records include Jane Birkin's own Birkin at about $10.05m, a Leica 0-series at €14.4m, and Prouvé, Perriand and Nakashima pieces at €0.5–1.2m+. But the watch market shows these assets are speculative: the WatchCharts overall index fell about 45% from its April 2022 peak by Nov 2025, and Subdial's Patek index was down 47% by Oct 2023. "Investment" framing turns craft objects into the same speculative assets as sneakers.

### Cited Findings
**Hermès Birkin**
- Jane Birkin's original 1985 Birkin sold at **Sotheby's Paris on 10 July 2025 for €8,582,500 (about $10.05m)**, the auction record for any handbag. The buyer, Valuence Japan, said it would treat the bag as cultural heritage rather than resell it. — [Sotheby's](https://www.sothebys.com/en/articles/world-record-hermes-birkin-sale); [Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2026/9/original-hermes-birkin-owned-by-actress-its-named-after-sells-for-record-10-million)
- **Resale premiums are compressing:** from about **2.2× retail at the 2022 peak to about 1.4–2× in late 2025**. This is reseller blog data. CNBC reports Birkin resale prices "leveled off" over two years and failed to keep pace with retail price rises, though they have **nearly doubled since 2015**. — [The Luxury Closet blog, May 2026](https://theluxurycloset.com/blog/2026/05/27/hermes-resale-value-2026-why-birkins-kellys-are-the-worlds-best-wearable-investment/); [MEXC repost of CNBC](https://www.mexc.com/news/282109)
- Privé Porter (a reseller) claims a **$298,000** private-sale record for a diamond-set Porosus crocodile Birkin 35. This is promotional and not an auction result. — [PurseBlog](https://www.purseblog.com/news/most-expensive-hermes-birkin/)

**Watches**
- **WatchCharts overall index:** about **$108,000 on 6 Nov 2025**, against nearly **$200,000 at the April 2022 peak**, so **about −45%**. Mid-2025 showed stabilisation, with Rolex and Patek indices up slightly (+0.1% in May 2025; the Rolex index +0.3% in Q1 2025). — [WatchCharts](https://watchcharts.com/articles/p/8961); [WatchCharts May 2025](https://watchcharts.com/articles/p/8617); [Quill & Pad June 2025](https://quillandpad.com/2025/06/21/watchcharts-june-2025-market-update-light-at-the-end-of-the-tunnel/)
- **Subdial (Oct 2023):** the Rolex index was **−27%** and the Patek Philippe index **−47%** from the April 2022 peak. WatchPro (early 2025) reported Patek prices still **+28% since the start of 2021**. The indices use different methods. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/retail/rolex-patek-prices-hit-fresh-two-year-lows-1.1699951073333); [WatchPro](https://usa.watchpro.com/?p=99987); [Fortune, July 2024](https://fortune.com/europe/2024/07/11/surge-in-pandemic-profits-rolex-patek-philippe-audemars-piguet-face-significant-downturn-sales-slip)

**Collectible design auctions**
- **Jean Prouvé:** a 1956 "Table Trapèze" made **€1,241,300** at Artcurial Paris (19 May, about 2014), twice its €600k high estimate and a record at the time. — [Artnet News](https://news.artnet.com/market/new-world-record-for-jean-prouve-23481?amp=1)
- **Charlotte Perriand:** "Bureau en forme" made **€560,000** at Artcurial (Oct 2017, timed to FIAC), beating the earlier **€420,000** record (Sotheby's Paris, 2009). The sale was 20 lots, all sold. — [Antiques Trade Gazette / The Saleroom, Nov 2017](https://content.the-saleroom.com/print-edition/2017/november/2316/international/all-lots-sold-and-record-set-in-auction-of-20-lots-by-designer-charlotte-perriand/)
- **George Nakashima:** the "Arlyn" table from the Krosnick collection made a record **$822,400** at Sotheby's (year not confirmed in the snippet). — [Antiques and The Arts Weekly](https://www.antiquesandthearts.com/records-set-for-greene-greene-george-nakashima-at-sothebys)
- **Pierre Jeanneret / Chandigarh:** a High Court lounge chair made **$47,500 at Wright (March 2011) against a $5–7k estimate**. Recent multiples are much lower: **8 Office Cane chairs for $22,860 (Rago, Jan 2025)** and **6 Punjab University library chairs for $7,860 (Wright, Aug 2023)**. An "Illuminated Library Table" is said to have made **$355,600 at Christie's, 10 June 2026** (single source, Masterworks, unverified). — [Wright 2011](https://www.wright20.com/auctions/2011/03/modern-design/117/print); [Rago 2025](https://www.ragoarts.com/auctions/2025/01/modern-design/197/print); [Wright 2023](https://www.wright20.com/auctions/2023/08/essential-design/125/print); [Masterworks](https://www.masterworks.com/academy/artists/pierre-jeanneret)

**Leica**
- **Leica 0-series no. 105** (Oskar Barnack's own camera, one of about 23 made in 1923–24) sold for **€14.4m including premium** at the 40th Leitz Photographica Auction, **11 June 2022**, against a **€2–3m estimate**. Leica calls it the world's most expensive camera. **De Sede DS-600 (Ueli Berger et al., 1972)**
- A 22-section cognac DS-600 made **$14,760** at Alderfer Auction. 1stDibs **asking** prices run from about **$20,000 to $85,000**, depending on number of sections, leather and restoration. Asking prices overstate realised prices. — [Antiques and The Arts Weekly](https://www.antiquesandthearts.com/alderfer-opens-september-with-twentieth-century-art-design); [1stDibs listing](https://www.1stdibs.com/furniture/seating/sofas/ds-600-ueli-berger-de-sede-stendig-section-sofa-22-sections/id-f_21472792)

**Vitsoe 606 (Dieter Rams, 1960)**
- Designed by Rams for Vitsoe in **1960**, it is sold as a reconfigurable "kit of parts". New components (for example, a recent stabilising foot) are **backwards-compatible** with existing systems. The X-Post was added in 1971, before which 606 was wall-mounted only. Vitsoe planners help customers reconfigure existing systems. — [Vitsoe, "stabilising foot"](https://www.vitsoe.com/gb/voice/stabilisingfoot); [Vitsoe 606](https://www.vitsoe.com/gb/606)
- Cambridge's Institute for Manufacturing uses Vitsoe as a case study in "timeless designs that encourage reuse" (page not fetched). — [IfM Cambridge](https://www.ifm.eng.cam.ac.uk/research/industrial-sustainability/resources/case-study-examples/vits)

### Inferences
- The highest prices attach to **provenance and authorship**: Jane Birkin's own bag, Barnack's own camera, Prouvé's own table. These are not material qualities. Most of the value comes from the object's biography (Kopytoff). That supports the argument that meaning accrues through use and history. It also shows that the price is a speculative bet on a story.
- Watches are the clearest warning. Rolex and Patek are durable, serviceable and well made, yet secondary prices fell 27–47% from the 2022 peak. Material honesty did not protect anyone who bought as an investment. Durability preserves usefulness, not market price.
- Jeanneret chairs started as anonymous, mass-ordered civic furniture. Their market value was created by Western dealers in the 2000s, and individual chairs now often fetch only a few thousand dollars. This is a revaluation through taste-making, close to hype in structure, and it raises extraction and heritage questions.
- Vitsoe offers a different model of value: the object is not expected to appreciate, but a system bought in the 1960s can still be extended and repaired with current parts. Value is kept through compatibility and use, not through resale. This is the strongest model for the student's thesis, though resale data is thin.

### Gaps
- No reliable Vitsoe 606 secondhand price data was found.
- Current (2025–2026) record prices for Pierre Chapo, Perriand, Prouvé and Nakashima were not found. The records cited may since have been surpassed. Check the Phillips, Christie's, Sotheby's and Wright results pages directly.
- Annual Phillips, Christie's and Sotheby's design-sale totals for 2024–2026 were not retrieved.
- No independent (non-Leica) confirmation of the €14.4m camera record was retrieved this session.

## 5. Why some objects hold value, and theory: Veblen, Bourdieu, Currid-Halkett, Kopytoff, Appadurai, Chapman, circular economy, mottainai

### Takeaway
Theory explains both sides. Veblen and Bourdieu explain why logo goods and quiet luxury alike work as status signals that inflate and collapse. Kopytoff and Appadurai explain how objects gain singular value through their biographies. Chapman and the Ellen MacArthur Foundation give the sustainability case for value through use: clothing use per garment fell 36% in 15 years while production doubled.

### Cited Findings
- **Veblen, *The Theory of the Leisure Class* (1899):** coined "conspicuous consumption", the display of wasteful expenditure as a marker of status. — [Project Gutenberg, ebook #833](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/833); also summarised in [Inside Higher Ed on Currid-Halkett](https://www.insidehighered.com/blogs/technology-and-learning/sum-small-things-inconspicuous-consumption-and-small-college-town)
- **Currid-Halkett (2017):** describes the shift from conspicuous to inconspicuous consumption among the "aspirational class". — [Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/9780691162737)
- **Chapman, *Emotionally Durable Design: Objects, Experiences and Empathy* (Earthscan, 2005; 2nd ed. Routledge 2015):** argues that making the user–product relationship more durable reduces consumption and waste. He targets causes, where recycling, biodegradability and design for disassembly treat symptoms. "Durability is as much about emotion, love and attachment as it is about fractured polymers, worn gaskets or blown circuitry". The book's core question is "why do we discard products that still work?" — [University of Brighton research portal](https://research.brighton.ac.uk/en/publications/emotionally-durable-design-objects-experiences-and-empathy/)
- **Ellen MacArthur Foundation, *A New Textiles Economy* (2017):** global clothing utilisation (the number of times a garment is worn before disposal) **fell 36% over 15 years**, and by **70% in China**, while **clothing sales more than doubled between 2000 and 2015**. About **one garbage truck of textiles is landfilled or burned every second**, and **more than $500bn of value is lost every year** through under-use and lack of recycling. — [Waste Dive](https://wastedive.com/news/report-a-garbage-truck-worth-of-textiles-are-incinerated-landfilled-every/511901); [Renewable Matter](https://www.renewablematter.eu/ellen-macarthur-foundation-lets-re-design-the-fashion-system)
- **Hermès** attributes its 2025 leather goods growth partly to "increase in production capacities", meaning new workshops. Its scarcity is therefore paced by how fast artisans can be trained, rather than by drop schedules. — [Hermès 2025 results](https://mfn.se/all/a/hermes/hermes-international-2025-full-year-results-a01f2a5e)

### Inferences
- **Framework for the student: what makes an object hold value:**
  1. **Material durability and repairability.** These keep use value (Vitsoe compatibility, watch servicing, a De Sede sofa reupholstered rather than discarded). They do not guarantee market price (the watch crash).
  2. **Authorship and provenance** (Prouvé, Nakashima, Jane Birkin's bag, Barnack's camera). These drive record prices. In Kopytoff's terms the object becomes "singularised".
  3. **Patina as proof of a lived biography.** Wear authenticates; a sneaker deadstock premium shows the reverse, since its value requires that the object was never used.
  4. **Supply limited by craft time.** Hermès workshops and Nakashima's woodshop limit output through labour, unlike drops (Supreme, the Nike vault). Craft-limited scarcity is still scarcity, and Hermès's quota and "purchase history" practices are a known critique (not sourced this session).
- **Where "appreciation" is itself speculation:** Birkin premiums compressing from 2.2× to 1.4–2×, the watch index down 45%, Chanel resale rising only because the list price rises, and Chandigarh chairs revalued by dealers all show that market appreciation behaves like any asset bubble. The defensible argument is that value should rise through **use value per unit of cost** (cost-per-wear), **repairability** and **personal or cultural meaning**, not through resale price. Auction records are evidence of the market for meaning, not proof that craft is a good investment.
- **Cost-per-wear illustration (calc., hypothetical):** a €1,000 coat worn 300 times over ten years costs about €3.33 per wear. A €100 fast-fashion coat worn 7 times costs about €14.30 per wear. The EMF data on falling utilisation makes this comparison relevant to the system, not only to the individual.
