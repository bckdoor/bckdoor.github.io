# Architecture, interiors, furniture and objects that show an inhabitant's personality through materiality, patina and joinery (not colour or branding)

Scope note: research done October 2026 using web search only (8 search rounds, about 45 queries). Many hits were dealer or auction listings (1stdibs, Chairish, Morentz). Those are fine for describing materials and construction, but their dates are unreliable, and every dealer-sourced date below is flagged. "Colour flag" marks cases where colour does strong identity work, against the brief. Each case uses the same fields: designer, year, place, materials, key joint/detail, ageing, what it says about the inhabitant, source.

## Key Question 1: Architecture and interiors. Which buildings and rooms express an inhabitant or client through material, patina and joinery?

### Takeaway
The best-documented cases are the ones where the making process stays visible in the finished building. Bruder Klaus has the charred imprint of burned logs and a hand-poured lead floor. At Klippan, no brick is cut and the mortar absorbs every difference. Vals is built from stacked local quartzite, Palmyra House from hand-cut palm louvres, and the YSL Museum from brick laid "like fabric". These split into three registers: ascetic (Zumthor, Lewerentz, Pawson, Ando), sensual or collecting (Vervoordt, Van Duysen, Bawa, Scarpa, Studio KO) and maker-inhabited (Esherick, Studio Mumbai). Barragán and E-1027 need colour caveats.

### Cited Findings

**1. Bruder Klaus Field Chapel.** Peter Zumthor; consecrated 19 May 2007; Mechernich-Wachendorf, Germany.
- Materials and detail: a tent of 112 tree trunks was encased in concrete poured and rammed in 50 cm layers. A smouldering fire burned inside for about three weeks (autumn 2006) until the dried trunks could be pulled out. That left a blackened cavity whose walls carry the imprint of the logs. — [Archiweb](https://www.archiweb.cz/en/b/polni-kaple-bruder-klaus); [Architectuul](https://architectuul.com/architecture/bruder-klaus-field-chapel)
- The floor is lead, melted on site in a crucible and ladled by hand. — [Archiweb](https://www.archiweb.cz/en/b/polni-kaple-bruder-klaus)
- Sources disagree on whether "24" means 24 layers or 24 working days. — [Arch2o](https://www.arch2o.com/?p=131762)
- Ageing: the char and soot marks are built in from day one. The patina comes first, then the building.
- Inhabitant: built for the farmer couple who commissioned it, as a devotional work to the hermit-saint Nicholas of Flüe. Persona: ascetic/contemplative.

**2. Therme Vals (now 7132 Thermal Baths).** Peter Zumthor; 1996; Vals, Graubünden, Switzerland.
- Materials: layer after layer of locally quarried Valser quartzite slabs, set partly into the hillside under a grass roof. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7132_Thermal_Baths); [RIBApix](https://www.architecture.com/image-library/ribapix/image-information/poster/therme-vals-hotel-and-spa-complex-graubunden-the-exterior-wall-masonry-in-valser-quarzite-slabs-of-t/posterid/RIBA46052.html)
- Detail: about 15 table-like stone units roughly 5 m high, with the circulation as the space left between blocks (the "meander"). Only one source gives this figure. — [Archello](https://archello.com/project/therme-vals)
- Ageing: the stone is wetted and worn by bathers' feet. Persona: ascetic/sensual bather. It is a public building, so the "inhabitant" is the bather.

**3. Atelier Zumthor (studio, with house nearby).** Peter Zumthor; 1986, concrete extension 2005; Haldenstein, Switzerland.
- Materials: a timber cuboid clad in thin larch slats with a "Japanese filigree" look, set against the rustic village. — [Chur Kultur](https://www.chur-kultur.ch/en/suche/haldenstein/atelier-zumthor_AfTZFTp); [Architectuul (Casa Z)](https://architectuul.com/architecture/casa-z)
- Ageing: no source found on how the larch has weathered (see Gaps). Persona: ascetic maker.

**4. St Peter's Church (S:t Petri).** Sigurd Lewerentz; 1966; Klippan, Sweden.
- Joint and detail: only standard bricks are used and none are cut, across walls, floors, ceilings and furniture. Mortar joints of varying size do the fitting, so the walls read "like a conglomerate". — [Adam Caruso essay (PDF)](https://carusostjohn.com/media/files/Adam_Caruso_-_Sigurd_Lewerentz_and_a_Material_Basis_for_Form.pdf)
- Lewerentz drawings keep the horizontal joints uniform while the vertical joints vary to absorb differences in brick size. — [OASE 105 (PDF)](https://www.oasejournal.nl/en/Downloads/5f4a619ff9582b6c85000004/OASE%20105%20-%2072%20Tekenen%20is%20bouwen.pdf)
- Patrick Lynch calls the refusal to cut a brick a "near-dogma". — [Lynch Architects](https://www.lyncharchitects.com/news-reviews/lewerentz/)
- Persona: ascetic and rule-bound. A self-imposed constraint becomes the character. The brief's best example of joinery as ethic.

**5. Home Farm.** John and Catherine Pawson; bought late 2012; Cotswolds, UK; farmhouse dating from 1610.
- Materials: mostly white marble, European elm and lime plaster. The house's own elm beams were milled into all the floorboards, doors and kitchen cupboards. The extension is layered tinted concrete, set against the Cotswold stone. — [The Modern House](https://themodernhouse.com/journal/the-classics-john-pawson-home-farm); [Sotheby's](https://www.sothebys.com/en/articles/catherine-and-john-pawsons-monastic-simplicity); [Wallpaper*](https://wallpaper.com/architecture/home-farm-john-pawson-cotswolds-uk)
- Pawson says oak would have been used in the grand house across the road. Elm suited a farm cottage, so the wood is chosen for its social register. — [Sotheby's](https://www.sothebys.com/en/articles/catherine-and-john-pawsons-monastic-simplicity)
- Correction to the brief: the material is elm, not oak.
- Persona: ascetic, but domestic and recycling. The house's own fabric is reused as its joinery.

**6. Nový Dvůr Monastery.** John Pawson with Jan Soukup; founded 2002, church consecrated September 2004; Dobrá Voda near Toužim, Bohemia, Czech Republic. A Trappist daughter house of Sept-Fons, built from a dilapidated Baroque manor that was partly kept and mostly replaced. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nov%C3%BD_Dv%C5%AFr_Monastery); [Archiweb](https://www.archiweb.cz/en/b/klasterni-kostel-trapistu-v-novem-dvore)
- Persona: the purest ascetic client, a community under a rule.

**7. Koshino House.** Tadao Ando; commissioned 1979, two parallel concrete volumes 1980–81, curved studio sunk into the ground 1983–84; Ashiya, Hyōgo, Japan. Client: fashion designer Hiroko Koshino.
- Detail: volumes partly sunk into a wooded slope and linked by an underground corridor; trees on the site were kept. — [ArchEyes](https://archeyes.com/?p=58719); [Metalocus](https://metalocus.es/en/news/dance-light-koshino-house-tadao-ando); [Arquitectura Viva](https://arquitecturaviva.com/works/54093)
- The source does not detail Ando's tie-hole grid, but it is well-known general knowledge (see Gaps).
- Persona: ascetic and contemplative. Light on concrete stands in for ornament.

**8. Kanaal.** Axel Vervoordt and partners; site bought 1998; Wijnegem, near Antwerp. A 19th-century malting distillery remade into 55,000 m² of housing, gallery and offices with Stéphane Beel, Coussée & Goris, Bogdan & Van Broeck, and landscape by Michel Desvigne. — [Chanintr](https://www.chanintr.com/pages/journal/a-wealth-of-art-and-nature/); [Bogdan & Van Broeck](https://www.bogdanvanbroeck.com/news/5399/)
- Founding dates conflict: 1857, per [Aficionados](https://theaficionados.com/journal/belgium/vervoordt-kanaal-antwerp), against 1863 with works from 1869, per [Wikipedia: Louis Meeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Meeus).
- Ageing: industrial brick and silos are kept with their age showing. Persona: warm host/collector. Patina is the thing being curated.

**9. Vincent Van Duysen's own house.** Vincent Van Duysen; 19th-century townhouse, a former notary office; Antwerp.
- Materials: poplar floors, rough Belgian linen, marble baths, handmade rugs, vegetable paints and plasters, furniture by Jeanneret, an inner courtyard with water. — [UTP Mag](https://utpmag.com/project/vincent-van-duysen); [Coggles](https://www.coggles.com/blog/interiors/inside-a-neutral-belgian-home)
- In his words, he exploits stone's "texture, its warmth, its imperfections and depth". — [Salvatori interview](https://www.salvatoriofficial.com/en/ww/stories/the-village-collection-interview-vincent-van-duysen)
- Persona: warm host, sensual minimalist.

**10. Lunuganga.** Geoffrey Bawa; bought January 1948 (Wikipedia says 1949) and worked on for about 50 years until his death in 2003; Bentota, Sri Lanka. A former rubber estate of 15 or 25 acres (sources conflict), developed with no master plan. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunuganga); [The Island](https://island.lk/a-visionary-garden-designed-with-such-care/)
- Ageing: built through slow accretion, and tropical weathering is part of the design. Persona: host/collector, with the landscape as autobiography. Now run by the Lunuganga Trust as a country-house hotel.

**11. Brion Tomb.** Carlo Scarpa; commissioned 1968 or 1969 by Onorina Tomasin-Brion; completed 1978; San Vito d'Altivole, Treviso, Italy.
- Materials and detail: concrete with mosaic, glass and bronze inserts and water. The interlocking twin-circle opening recurs from window scale down to tiny details. Scarpa is buried on the site. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brion_tomb); [Port Magazine](https://www.port-magazine.com/?p=80580)
- Persona: a marriage and its memory, expressed through obsessive detail. Collector-sensual register.

**12. Musée Yves Saint Laurent Marrakech.** Studio KO (Karl Fournier, Olivier Marty); opened 2017; Marrakech, Morocco.
- Materials and detail: locally made terracotta brick from Moroccan earth, laid so the facade recalls the warp and weft of fabric or lace. White interior walls are meant to evoke a couture jacket's lining. — [Wallpaper*](https://wallpaper.com/fashion/yves-saint-laurent-museum-marrakech); [Architectural Record](https://www.architecturalrecord.com/articles/13170-mus%C3%A9e-yves-saint-laurent-by-studio-ko); [museum press release (PDF)](https://www.museeyslmarrakech.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Musee-YSL-Marrakech-Studio-KO_Press-Release_May-2016-1.pdf)
- Persona: the client (YSL's legacy) is portrayed through tailoring logic in masonry, with no logo. A good counter-example to branding.

**13. Palmyra House.** Studio Mumbai (Bijoy Jain); 2007; Nandgaon, Alibag area, Maharashtra, India; about 278 m².
- Materials and joint: louvres hand-cut from the outer part of local palmyra palm trunks, on an ain-hardwood frame with traditional interlocking joinery. — [Aga Khan Award](https://www.akdn.org/architecture/project/palmyra-house); [Architecture Lab](https://architecturelab.net/palmyra-house-studio-mumbai-architects)
- Persona: nomadic/maker. The studio is built around craftsmen who make the work themselves.

**14. Fisher House.** Louis Kahn; 1967; Hatboro, Pennsylvania. Two cypress-clad cubes ("living" and "sleeping") on local Montgomeryville stone, built for Dr Norman and Doris Fisher. It is one of nine surviving Kahn houses and was deeded to the National Trust in 2011. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher_House_(Hatboro,_Pennsylvania)); [Docomomo US](https://docomomo-us.org/register/fisher-house)
- Ageing of the cypress was not documented in the sources found. Persona: contemplative family.

**15. Wharton Esherick House and Studio.** Wharton Esherick; land 1913, studio begun 1926, spiral stair 1930; Paoli, Pennsylvania; now a National Historic Landmark.
- Joint and detail: a spiral stair of massive hand-hewn red-oak treads keyed into an old tree trunk, with a mastodon-tusk handhold. It was removed and reinstalled for the 1939 World's Fair and again in 1958. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wharton_Esherick); [Wharton Esherick Museum](https://whartonesherickmuseum.org/about/)
- Persona: maker. The house is the maker's lifelong self-portrait, kept as he left it.

**16. Casa Luis Barragán. Colour flag.** Luis Barragán; 1947–48; Tacubaya, Mexico City; UNESCO World Heritage 2004.
- Materials: concrete with plaster render, per [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/document/151840). Secondary sources add volcanic stone, pine floors and beams, and gold leaf. — [Studio Matrx](https://www.studiomatrx.org/architecture-canon/casa-luis-barragan)
- Colour does strong identity work: magenta stair, yellow beams, pink entry. — [Design Hotels](https://www.designhotels.com/directions/design/the-technicolor-corners-of-barragan-s-mexico-city/)

**17. E-1027. Colour/mural flag.** Eileen Gray with Jean Badovici; 1926–29; Roquebrune-Cap-Martin, France. Le Corbusier painted murals there in 1938–39 (counts of 7 or 8), which Gray called vandalism. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-1027); [Domus](https://domusweb.it/en/speciali/novanta-anni/2018/le-corbusiers-violation-of-eileen-grays-work.html)
- Use it as a cautionary tale: a later imposition of image and colour overwrote the client's or designer's material identity.

**18. Rick Owens and Michèle Lamy, home and furniture.** Started 2007, for their Paris home and atelier. Materials include plywood, marble, alabaster, bronze, ox bone, leather, concrete and moose antler, as in the Stag stools with a single antler for a back. Lamy is credited by MOCA as the primary creative force and producer. — [MOCA brochure (PDF)](https://www.moca.org/storage/app/media/Brochures/010417_RO_Brochure_G4_REV_Singlepage.pdf); [MOCA exhibition](https://www.moca.org/exhibition/rickowensfurniture)
- Persona: nomadic/maker with a brutalist, punk edge. Identity carried by raw mass, not colour.

### Inferences
- **Material "honesty" works as personality** when a single rule is visible: no cut bricks, burned formwork, the house's own beams reused, palm trunks shaved on site. Each rule implies the inhabitant's values: discipline, devotion, thrift, a link to place.
- **Patina can come at three times.** It can be built in at day zero (Bruder Klaus char), it can accrue (Vals stone, Lunuganga, Kanaal's kept industrial fabric), or it can be curated or imported (Vervoordt, Van Duysen using Jeanneret pieces). This maps cleanly onto the personas: ascetic (built in), host/collector (curated), maker (accrued through use).
- **Barragán and E-1027 should be used as contrast cases.** In Barragán, colour is inseparable from material, because it is pigment on rough render. E-1027 shows the risk of a totalising imposed image, which fits the "without totalising" theme.

### Gaps
- Not verified with sources in this pass: Castelvecchio (Scarpa, 1959–74), Querini Stampalia (1961–63), Negozio Olivetti (1957–58), Pawson House London (1999), 's-Gravenwezel castle, Vervoordt/Ando "Garden of Silence" at Kanaal, Villa Mabrouka (Studio KO for YSL/Bergé, Tangier), Kengo Kuma, Valerio Olgiati, Kahn's Esherick House (1959–61). Dates are from training knowledge only, so verify before use.
- No source found on how the Haldenstein larch or the Fisher House cypress has actually weathered.
- Ando's tie-hole/formwork detail at Koshino is not cited.
- Brion "stepped ziggurat" profiles: no source confirmed.

## Key Question 2: Furniture, including sofas. Which pieces carry personality through joinery, leather and wood ageing?

### Takeaway
Furniture splits into two groups. Some pieces age into character: Chapo elm, Mogensen saddle leather, Nakashima walnut with butterfly keys, Jeanneret teak and cane. Others reveal it through visible assembly logic: Mari's nailed boards, Judd's slip-together plywood, De Sede's zip-hinge leather elements, Bellini's hooks and rings, the Soriana's external steel clamp. Sofas lean toward the host persona, and hand-made solid-wood pieces toward the maker and ascetic personas.

### Cited Findings

**19. De Sede DS-600 "Non-Stop"/"Snake" sofa.** Ueli Berger, Eleonore Peduzzi-Riva, Heinz Ulrich, Klaus Vogt (some listings omit Vogt) with the De Sede team; 1972; Klingnau, Switzerland. In continuous production, with an outdoor version in 2018.
- Joint: each element is an L-shaped hard-foam core with a down-filled leather cushion. Elements are joined by a plug-in hinge and a hide-and-zipper connection, so the joint is itself leather. — [1stdibs listing](https://www.1stdibs.com/furniture/seating/sofas/1972-de-sede-ds-600-snake-sectional-sofa-cognac-brown-leather-sofa-switzerland/id-f_49255942/); [Domus](https://domusweb.it/en/product-news/2018/08/10/ds-600-the-outdoor-version-of-the-worlds-longest-sofa.amp.html)
- The claim of a 406-element installation in a sultan's palace is marketing copy and unverified. — [Architonic](https://www.architonic.com/en/p/ds-600-snake-1049757)
- Ageing: thick leather creases and burnishes. Persona: host. The sofa can grow with the household and the gathering.

**20. Camaleonda.** Mario Bellini for C&B/B&B Italia; 1970, reissued by B&B Italia (dealer dates for individual pieces vary, 1971–79).
- Joint: 90×90 cm modules tufted (capitonné) with tie-rods, hooks and rings; the reissue uses chromed brass rings and a steel carabiner. Modules can be unhooked and recombined. Shown at MoMA's "Italy: The New Domestic Landscape", 1972. — [MOHD](https://shop.mohd.it/en/camaleonda-modular-sofa.html); [Innes](https://innes.co.uk/collections/b-b-italia-camaleonda-modular-sofa)
- Persona: host and nomad. A reconfigurable "landscape".

**21. Soriana.** Afra and Tobia Scarpa for Cassina; 1969; Compasso d'Oro 1970. Attribution note: the brief names only Tobia, but the design is credited to the couple.
- Detail: an external chrome-plated steel clamp whose tension shapes the polyurethane and fibre upholstery, with no rigid inner frame. — [NGV essay](https://www.ngv.vic.gov.au/essay/gli-scarpa); [Chairish](https://www.chairish.com/product/29024181/soriana-sofa-by-afra-and-tobia-scarpa-for-cassina-1970)
- Persona: host and sensualist.

**22. Spanish Chair.** Børge Mogensen for Fredericia; 1958 (one listing says 1950); in production.
- Materials and joint: a solid oak frame (some runs in walnut) with thick saddle-leather seat and back on adjustable straps and brass buckles. Current versions use vegetable-tanned leather. The name refers to a Spanish/Moorish technique of saddle leather on a wooden frame. — [Finnish Design Shop](https://www.finnishdesignshop.com/en-dk/product/the-spanish-chair-black-leather-oiled-walnut); [1stdibs](https://www.1stdibs.com/designs/borge-mogensen-spanish-chair/)
- Ageing: dealers describe "age-appropriate patina" on frame and leather, and the straps can be re-tensioned as the leather stretches. Persona: host/collector, sociable and tactile.

**23. S11 chair (and Chapo's work in general).** Pierre Chapo; 1966 by most dealers (some say 1970s); Gordes, France.
- Materials and joint: solid elm with a leather seat and back laced underneath with cord. Exposed joinery, with one dealer describing a deliberately missing angle where the seat meets the leg. — [Chairish](https://www.chairish.com/product/31965364/s11-chair-in-whisky-leather-by-pierre-chapo-1960s); [Morentz](https://www.morentz.com/products/early-pierre-chapo-s11-dining-chair-in-elm-and-cognac-leather); [1stdibs](https://www.1stdibs.com/furniture/seating/dining-room-chairs/pierre-chapo-s11-dining-chairs-atelier-pierre-chapo-1966-set-of-2/id-f_46518102)
- Ageing: elm darkens to honey, and the leather takes on a cognac patina. Persona: maker/ascetic, rural.

**24. Conoid line.** George Nakashima; introduced 1961 (the brief's "1960" is not confirmed), named after the Conoid Studio of 1957–59; New Hope, Pennsylvania.
- Materials and joint: walnut seats and slabs with hickory spindles shaved by hand with a block plane (Windsor lineage). Butterfly keys, often rosewood, join bookmatched slabs and stop cracks spreading, so the flaw becomes a feature. — [Phillips](https://www.phillips.com/detail/george-nakashima/121581); [Moderne Gallery](https://modernegallery.com/works/furniture/tables/dining-tables/george-nakashima-conoid-dining-table-1984/); [1stdibs](https://www.1stdibs.com/designs/george-nakashima-conoid-chair)
- Persona: maker/contemplative. Nakashima's own reverence for the tree.

**25. Autoprogettazione.** Enzo Mari; 1974. Plans for 18 or 19 pieces (counts differ) built from standard lumber with hammer and nails, mailed free on request. Artek put Sedia 1 into production as a pine kit in 2010. It was explicitly framed as a critique of consumerist mass production and as an education. — [Core77](https://www.core77.com/posts/17283/Enzo-Mari-on-Film-Arteks-Short-Documentary-of-Autoprogettazione); [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2010/04/16/sedia-1-chair-by-enzo-mari-for-artek/amp/)
- Persona: nomadic/maker. The owner's own hand is the joint.

**26. Donald Judd furniture.** Donald Judd; from about 1970 (Fifth Floor Bed, 1970); 101 Spring Street, New York, and Marfa, Texas.
- Materials and joint: rough lumberyard pine and birch plywood, slip-together designs made with as few cuts as possible. Early Marfa pieces came from "want of local alternatives". Some early plywood chairs had visible screws, which were later dropped. — [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2024/06/22/donald-judd-furniture-key-designs-table-bed/amp/); [Judd Foundation](https://juddfoundation.org/program/donald-judd-furniture-wood-and-metal/); [Phillips](https://phillips.com/detail/donald-judd/UK050222/54)
- Persona: ascetic. Note the brief's conflation: 101 Spring Street is in New York, not Marfa.

**27. Pierre Jeanneret, Chandigarh furniture.** Pierre Jeanneret; about 1955–57; Chandigarh, India. Teak and woven cane, for example the PJ-SI-59 low easy chair of about 1955.
- Market: a pair sold for £30,000 at Bonhams in October 2017. Six-figure results are now common, partly because much of the furniture was thrown away rather than restored. — [Bonhams](https://www.bonhams.com/auction/24238/lot/41/pierre-jeanneret-a-pair-of-pj-si-59-low-easy-chairs-circa-1955-designed-for-chandigarh-teak-woven-cane-65cm-x-55cm-x-73cm); [Cheffins](https://fineart.cheffins.co.uk/news-item/trendspotter--pierre-jeanneret-for-chandigarh/)
- India imposed a "soft ban" on exports in 2011. — [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/chandigarh/no-stopping-auctions-chandigarhs-heritage-up-for-grabs-down-under-302250)
- A claimed record of US$355,600 (Christie's, 10 June 2026) comes from a single source and is unverified. — [Masterworks](https://www.masterworks.com/artists/pierre-jeanneret)
- Persona: collector. Patina here is also provenance capital, which risks turning patina into branding.

**28. Les Arcs.** Charlotte Perriand; 1967 to the mid-1980s (sources give end dates of 1984, 1985 or 1989); Savoie, France. Prefabricated bathroom pods "plugged in" to the building frames, plus solid pine benches, tables and shelves. One shelf type was first made in 1952 for her own Paris flat. — [Art Institute of Chicago](https://www.artic.edu/articles/639/finding-perriand); [Proantic](https://www.proantic.com/en/1835391-pine-wall-shelf-les-arcs-1800-circa-1970.html)
- Persona: nomadic (holiday living, collective, mountain).

**29. Thinking Man's Chair.** Jasper Morrison for Cappellini; 1986 prototype (V&A gives 1987 design and 1989 manufacture; Cappellini says 1988).
- Materials and detail: tubular and strip steel with high-quality welding. The armrests end in discs for drinks. The prototype was hand-sprayed with red-oxide primer. — [V&A](https://api.vam.ac.uk/v2/object/O113578); [Contemporary Art Society](https://contemporaryartsociety.org/node/6284)
- Persona: the solitary thinker. The rust-primer colour is a material marker, not a brand colour.

**30. Roly-Poly chair.** Faye Toogood; April 2014 (Assemblage 4, Project B Gallery, Milan). Started as a clay maquette and was hand-cast in fibreglass by a small British boat-type maker in raw, charcoal or cream. Rounded feet are linked to her pregnancy and motherhood. Later editions in aluminium and gold leaf, and a Driade version. Held by the Philadelphia Museum of Art and the NGV. — [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2014/04/04/faye-toogood-assemblage-4-furniture-milan-2014/); [Toogood](https://t-o-o-g-o-o-d.com/pages/roly-poly-chair-email)
- Persona: warm host, bodily.

**31. Max Lamb.** Pewter stools sand-cast on a Cornish beach (around 2006, after his RCA graduation). Separately, the Poly works (2011) in carved polystyrene and rubber, some lost-foam cast in bronze. — [MR PORTER](https://www.mrporter.com/en-gb/journal/lifestyle/the-chair-men-355234); [Nuvo](https://nuvomagazine.com/magazine/summer-2015/max-lamb)
- The brief's "Poly chair cast in pewter" merges two different bodies of work. Persona: nomadic maker. The site becomes the mould.

### Inferences
- **Sofas are the host's instrument.** DS-600, Camaleonda and Soriana are all modular or tension-based, and they show sociability through how they are assembled: zips, hooks, clamps. Leather (DS-600, Mogensen) is the main ageing medium.
- **Solid wood with exposed joints (Chapo, Nakashima, Esherick, Judd, Mari) suits the maker/ascetic personas.** Butterfly keys are the clearest example of "repair as signature".
- **The Jeanneret market shows patina being commodified into a brand signal.** This is a useful caution for a brief that rejects branding.

### Gaps
- Chapo's Godot bed: no source found for its date or construction.
- Not researched with sources in this pass: Cassina LC / I Maestri, Ilse Crawford/Studioilse, Super Normal (Morrison/Fukasawa, 2006), Wegner, Klint, Sam Maloof, Christian Liaigre, Pierre Yovanovitch, De Sede leather craft beyond the DS-600.
- Nakashima: no museum source confirmed the Conoid introduction year. The 1961 date comes from dealers.

## Key Question 3: Objects. Which everyday objects carry personality through wear and patina?

### Takeaway
The objects show that patina is, at bottom, ownership: a record of one person's handling. Kaikado caddies are polished by stroking. Black-paint Leicas brass at the user's grip points. Nambu kettles build up a mineral film from the owner's own water. The Vitsoe 606 instead shows identity through reconfigurability over a lifetime.

### Cited Findings

**32. Kaikado chazutsu (tea caddies).** Kaikado, founded 1875; Kyoto. Designs essentially unchanged since then.
- Materials and detail: copper, brass or tin, double-walled with a tin-plate inner, and an airtight lid that sinks shut under its own weight. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaikado); [Yoshien](https://yoshien.com/en/tea-caddy-kaikado-brass-100g.html)
- Ageing: uncoated metal develops a patina, and Ippodo advises stroking it to help. — [Ippodo](https://global.ippodo-tea.co.jp/products/utensils902036)
- Persona: contemplative/host.

**33. Leica M, black paint.** Ernst Leitz/Leica, Wetzlar.
- Detail: black paint over a brass top plate wears through to brass ("brassing"). Black chrome wears to silver-grey because there is nickel underneath. Some models (the M6 and most M4-P) have zinc tops and do not brass. Wear rates vary with handling. — [L-Camera Forum](https://www.l-camera-forum.com/topic/268459-black-chrome-finish/page/2); [L-Camera Forum](https://www.l-camera-forum.com/topic/282528-m10-brasses-quickly/)
- These are user reports, not Leica specifications. Persona: nomad/maker (the working photographer).

**34. Nambu tekki (cast-iron kettles).** Morioka and Iwate, Japan.
- Ageing: kettles are fired at 800–1,000 °C ("kama-yaki") to form an oxide layer. Seasoning by boiling hard water down builds a mineral film that must not be scrubbed. Red rust is harmless and can be treated by boiling tea leaves. A kettle is meant to last decades or generations. — [Musubi Kiln](https://musubikiln.com/pages/how-to-care-for-nambu-ironware)
- Persona: contemplative. The ritual of care is itself the patina.

**35. Vitsoe 606 Universal Shelving System.** Dieter Rams for Vitsœ; 1960 per Vitsoe (Icon says 1959). A kit of parts that is reconfigured over a lifetime. Vitsoe planners encourage buying less at first. — [Vitsoe](https://www.vitsoe.com/gb/606); [Remodelista](https://www.remodelista.com/posts/vitsoe-606-universal-shelving-worlds-greenest-and-most-economical-system)
- Persona: nomad (it moves house with its owner) and ascetic.

### Inferences
- **The objects give the most direct link between a user's habits and the material.** The wear pattern is literally the person: grip, tea ritual, local water. They are good "proof" artefacts for each persona board.
