## 1. Wagner's origin of the term (1849): what he meant, and how it was totalising

### Takeaway
Wagner did not coin the word, but he made it famous in his Zurich-exile essays of 1849 (*Die Kunst und die Revolution*, *Das Kunstwerk der Zukunft*). He meant a revolutionary, communal artwork, made by a "fellowship" of artists for and by the *Volk*. It reunites the separated arts (dance, music, poetry, with architecture, sculpture and painting serving the drama) on the model of Attic tragedy. Its totalising side comes from three things: the arts are subordinated to a single dramatic end, a single master-author controls them (Bayreuth), and spectators are absorbed into the work. Adorno read that absorption as proto-fascist. Koss argues the 1849 version actually asked for active, communal spectatorship.

### Cited Findings
- Wagner wrote *Das Kunstwerk der Zukunft* in Zurich exile in 1849, alongside *Die Kunst und die Revolution*. He first used "Gesamtkunstwerk" for Attic tragedy in *Die Kunst und die Revolution*, then extended it to a utopian programme for a future artwork. That artwork could arise only as the work of a cooperative of all artists (*Genossenschaft aller Künstler*). [VERIFIED-SNIPPET] — [Das Kunstwerk der Zukunft, de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Das_Kunstwerk_der_Zukunft); [See This Sound – Kompendium "Gesamtkunstwerk"](http://www.see-this-sound.at/kompendium/text/41/4.html)
- Scholarly reading: the 1849 essay imagines a Gesamtkunstwerk *by and for the people*. Strictly, the term belongs to Wagner's politically motivated plans of 1849, as distinct from his later *Oper und Drama* (1851) and the "music drama". [VERIFIED-SNIPPET] — [Sanna Pederson, "From Gesamtkunstwerk to Music Drama" (PDF, hcommons)](https://hcommons.org/app/uploads/sites/1001239/2018/11/gesamtkunstwerk-Pederson.pdf); see also [bpb.de, "Wagners politisch-ästhetische Utopie und ihre Interpretation" (APuZ)](https://www.bpb.de/shop/zeitschriften/apuz/160061/wagners-politisch-aesthetische-utopie-und-ihre-interpretation/)
- Wagner's total art has been read as a communal form of belonging, first for Germans and later for humanity, pursued through art. That is the political charge behind its "totalising" reputation. [VERIFIED-SNIPPET] — [bpb.de APuZ](https://www.bpb.de/shop/zeitschriften/apuz/160061/wagners-politisch-aesthetische-utopie-und-ihre-interpretation/)
- English text: the standard older translation is William Ashton Ellis, *Richard Wagner's Prose Works*, vol. 1 (London: Kegan Paul, 1895; repr. as *The Art-Work of the Future and Other Works*, Univ. of Nebraska Press, 1993). A new translation by Emma Warner appeared in 2013. One commentator calls Ellis "notoriously impenetrable". [VERIFIED-SNIPPET] — [IMSLP, Richard Wagner's Prose Works](https://imslp.org/wiki/Richard_Wagner%27s_Prose_Works_(Wagner,_Richard)); [biblio.com Nebraska 1993 reprint](https://www.biblio.com/0803297521); [The Wagnerian, 2013](https://www.the-wagnerian.com/2013/05/new-translation-of-artwork-of-future.html)
- Critique, Adorno: *Versuch über Wagner* (written 1937–38, published 1952; Eng. *In Search of Wagner*). Adorno argued that the Wagnerian total work is a commodity-like phantasmagoria that hides its own production and encourages passive audience submission. A review of Koss summarises his view as: fascism had roots in Wagner's total theatre. [VERIFIED-SNIPPET for the summary] — [Goodreads, In Search of Wagner](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/1769458); [Rain Taxi review of Koss](https://raintaxi.com/modernism-after-wagner/)
- Counter-reading, Koss: *Modernism after Wagner* (Minneapolis: University of Minnesota Press, ©2010; 381 pp.; ISBN 9780816651580). Chapters: "The Utopian Gesamtkunstwerk", "Building Bayreuth", "Empathy Abstracted", "The Nietzschean Festival", "Retheatricalizing the Theater", "The Specter of Cinema", "Bauhaus Theater of Human Dolls", "Invisible Wagner". Koss argues that Adorno's reading conflates the *active communal spectatorship* Wagner demanded in 1849 with the *passive* mass spectatorship of fascism, and she calls Adorno's analysis "ahistorical, tendentious, and misleading" (reviewer's paraphrase). She centres the spectator's active co-creation of the artwork. [VERIFIED-SNIPPET] — [Rain Taxi](https://raintaxi.com/modernism-after-wagner/); [Project MUSE review](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/426556); [Leonardo review (dates it 2009)](https://leonardo.info/reviews_archive/feb2012/blassnigg-koss.php); [Scripps College feature](https://scrippscollege.edu/news/features/modernism-after-wagner); [CCA library record](https://www.cca.qc.ca/en/search/details/library/publication/310959652)
- First use of the word: the term "Gesamtkunstwerk" is usually traced to the philosopher K. F. E. Trahndorff, *Ästhetik oder Lehre von Weltanschauung und Kunst* (1827). [FROM MEMORY – VERIFY]
- Wagner on architecture: in *Das Kunstwerk der Zukunft*, architecture, sculpture and painting serve the drama. Architecture's highest task is the theatre building, which exists for the performance (later realised as the Bayreuth Festspielhaus, opened 1876). Ellis's English renders the term with phrases like "the great united Art-work". [FROM MEMORY – VERIFY; the phrase "great united Art-work" could NOT be found in any search result this session]

### Inferences
- For the student's argument, Koss's reading is the useful lever. The Gesamtkunstwerk began (1849) as a collaborative, communal and spectator-activated idea. The *single-author, total-control* version (Bayreuth, then Adorno's critique) is one historical path, not the definition. A "Gesamtkunstwerk without totalising" can claim to be closer to the 1849 "fellowship" ideal than to Bayreuth.
- The totalising features to name: (a) one governing end (the drama, or the architect's style) that all parts must serve; (b) one controlling author; (c) the immersed, passive recipient.

## 2. Practice of total design c. 1895–1911: Hoffmann / Wiener Werkstätte, van de Velde, Behrens, Mackintosh, Wright

### Takeaway
Around 1900 architect-designers carried total design from the facade to the cutlery, and in several cases to the clothes of the inhabitants. Van de Velde designed dresses for his wife at Bloemenwerf. Hoffmann reportedly did the same for Madame Stoclet. Wright designed furniture, glass and textiles for his clients' houses. Behrens extended the idea from the home to the corporation at AEG (1907). These are the core "totalising" precedents Loos attacked.

## 3. Adolf Loos: "The Poor Little Rich Man" (1900), "Ornament and Crime" (1910), Raumplan, and interiors that grow with their inhabitants

### Takeaway
Loos's "Poor Little Rich Man" (*Vom armen reichen Manne*, *Neues Wiener Tagblatt*, 26 April 1900) satirises the Secessionist/Hoffmann-style total interior. Once the architect has completed the house, the owner may not add, move or change anything, even his slippers, and so is "complete", shut out of future life. It is the founding critique of the domestic Gesamtkunstwerk and the most important text for the student's position. "Ornament and Crime" was a lecture of 1910, not 1908. Loos's alternative: neutral, well-made architecture, with interiors that accumulate the inhabitants' own belongings and memories over time.

### Cited Findings
- Plot: a wealthy man commissions an architect to put art into his whole house. The architect designs everything. Later the architect returns, scolds him for misplacing things, and explodes over the client wearing slippers designed for the bedroom in another room (or wearing his own, non-designed slippers). The ending says the client is shut out of "future life and its strivings, its developments, and its desires". The essay is aimed at Josef Hoffmann / the Secession. [VERIFIED-SNIPPET] — [Reading Design, "The Obligation to Self-Design" (Boris Groys essay, citing Loos)](https://www.readingdesign.org/obligation-to-self-design); [Apollo Magazine, "The business of luxury in modern Vienna"](https://apollo-magazine.com/the-business-of-luxury-in-modern-vienna/); [Taylor & Francis anthology chapter, "The Poor Little Rich Man"](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/edit/10.4324/9781315816852-19/poor-little-rich-man-adolf-loos); text on [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/338141775/Adolf-Loos-The-Poor-Little-Rich-Man-1900)
- Commonly quoted English ending (Jane O. Newman and John H. The architect forbids any additions, including presents from family, which must be cleared with him. [FROM MEMORY – VERIFY exact wording]
- Groys (on Reading Design) uses Loos to show how total design turns the inhabitant into an exhibit of the designer's work. The client is subject to an "obligation" to stay as designed. ### Inferences
- Loos gives the student the two halves of the argument. (1) The critique: total design petrifies the client into a finished exhibit. - Irony worth noting: Loos himself designed many of his clients' interiors in full. His position is less "never design the interior" than "do not design the inhabitant's life".

## 4. Bauhaus: Gropius's 1919 manifesto ("the building as final aim") and modernist total design

### Takeaway
Gropius's April 1919 Bauhaus manifesto (with Feininger's "Cathedral" woodcut) renews the Gesamtkunstwerk as a collective, craft-based "building" that unifies architecture, sculpture and painting. English wording varies between translations, so the student must quote one named translation.

### Cited Findings
- Opening line in various translations: Getty: *"The ultimate goal of all creative activity is the building!"*; Bauhaus Dessau journal: *"The ultimate goal of all artistic activity is the building!"*; others: *"The ultimate aim of all visual arts is the complete building!"* (German: *"Das Endziel aller bildnerischen Tätigkeit ist der Bau!"* [German FROM MEMORY – VERIFY]). Text by Walter Gropius, 1919; woodcut by Lyonel Feininger, 1919. [VERIFIED-SNIPPET] — [Getty Research Institute, Bauhaus exhibition history](https://www.getty.edu/research/exhibitions_events/exhibitions/bauhaus/new_artist/history/); [Bauhaus Dessau Atlas, "Crafting Knowledge" intro](https://atlas-mobile.bauhaus-dessau.de/en/journal/crafting-knowledge-craft-as-an-epistemic-practice-2/introduction-crafting-knowledge); [bauhaus imaginista, "the bauhaus manifesto"](https://www.bauhaus-imaginista.org/articles/1771/the-bauhaus-manifesto/); [Gropius House, Bauhaus Manifesto 1919](https://gropius.house/?p=6028)
- The manifesto says there is no essential difference between artist and craftsman. [VERIFIED-SNIPPET] — [Bauhaus Dessau Atlas](https://atlas-mobile.bauhaus-dessau.de/en/journal/crafting-knowledge-craft-as-an-epistemic-practice-2/introduction-crafting-knowledge)
- Its closing image: a new building of the future that will combine architecture, sculpture and painting "in one unity" and "rise toward heaven from the hands of a million workers like the crystal symbol of a new faith". [FROM MEMORY – VERIFY; standard in the Hans Wingler, *The Bauhaus*, MIT Press 1969 translation]
- Koss's chapter "Bauhaus Theater of Human Dolls" follows the Wagnerian lineage into Bauhaus stage experiments (Schlemmer). [VERIFIED-SNIPPET for chapter title] — [Rain Taxi](https://raintaxi.com/modernism-after-wagner/)

### Inferences
- The 1919 Bauhaus is the *collective, workshop* Gesamtkunstwerk: anonymous craftsmen, not one genius. That is closer to Wagner's 1849 "fellowship" and to the student's idea of a coordinated team serving a client. The later Bauhaus/International Style legacy (standardisation, "total design" of living) brought the totalising problem back at the scale of mass housing.

## 5. Contemporary critiques: Foster, Wigley, Colomina, Koss, Groys, Eco, Sennett, Brand, Morrison/Fukasawa

### Takeaway
Hal Foster's "Design and Crime" (2002) is the key critique. It re-reads Loos's parable to argue that the Art Nouveau dream of total design has now come true as consumer branding, leaving no "running room" for subjects. Eco (open work), Brand (buildings that learn), Sennett (craft as an open, repairable process), and Morrison/Fukasawa (the unassertive "super normal") supply the positive theory for a total environment that stays open to its inhabitant and to time.

### Cited Findings

- "Design and Crime" is chapter 2 (p. 13) of *Design and Crime (And Other Diatribes)* (London/New York: Verso, 2002). A Portuguese translation appeared in *Ars* (USP) in 2011. [VERIFIED-SNIPPET] — [Ars (USP), "Design e crime"](https://revistas.usp.br/ars/article/view/52784); [Beccari, "From ornament to total design: a historic overview from Hal Foster", *Palíndromo* (UDESC), 2020](https://periodicos.udesc.br/index.php/palindromo/en/article/view/16824)

## 6. Precedents for a non-totalising total work (the inhabitant/time completes the work)

### Takeaway
Eileen Gray's E-1027 (1926–29) is the strongest documented precedent: a total house and furniture designed around the occupants' bodies and habits, then itself a victim of an authorial "total" act (Le Corbusier's murals). Perriand, Scarpa, sukiya architecture and Vervoordt are plausible further precedents, but this session could not source specific claims about them.

### Cited Findings
- E-1027, Roquebrune-Cap-Martin, by Eileen Gray (with Jean Badovici), 1926–29. Designed for herself and Badovici, with furniture and fittings by Gray. After their 1932 split Badovici kept the house. Le Corbusier painted murals there in 1938–39 against Gray's wishes. ### Inferences
- E-1027 makes a neat closing case. Gray's total design was *anthropocentric*, built around use and habit, while the totalising gesture came from another architect overwriting it. The same house shows the difference between total *care* and total *control*.
- Synthesised position for the student: a "Gesamtkunstwerk without totalising" = (1) coherence at the slow layers (structure, space, material palette, craft standard), designed by a collaborative team (1849 Wagner "fellowship" / 1919 Bauhaus workshop); (2) openness at the fast layers (objects, clothing, arrangement), co-authored with the client and left to time (Loos, Brand, Eco); (3) an ethic of restraint and the ordinary (Super Normal, Sennett), so the client's personality, not the designer's signature, is what is expressed.
