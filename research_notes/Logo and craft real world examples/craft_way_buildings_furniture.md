## Houses / exteriors: which real buildings best show "precision holds patina"?

### Takeaway
The strongest exterior examples with free photos are Zumthor's **Bruder Klaus Field Chapel** (charred-timber void inside rammed concrete), **Therme Vals** (stacked Valser quartzite), **Lewerentz's St Peter's, Klippan** (whole uncut bricks with thick mortar joints) and **Fujimori's Takasugi-an / Yakisugi House** (charred cedar). **Lunuganga** and the **Esherick and Nakashima compounds** add the lived-in, slowly accreted version. All of these have Commons files with confirmed licences, except the Vals photos, whose licences are unconfirmed.

### Cited Findings

**1. Bruder Klaus Field Chapel (Bruder-Klaus-Feldkapelle)**: Peter Zumthor, 2005–2007, Mechernich-Wachendorf, Eifel, Germany. Scene: house/exterior, and a single-object close-up of the material.
- Built around a tent of 112 spruce trunks. Concrete was rammed around them in roughly 50 cm layers by volunteers over 24 days. A smouldering fire then burned the wood out, and the charred trunks were removed, leaving a blackened negative of the logs inside. The floor is a poured tin-lead alloy. Sources disagree on whether the fire burned for 2 or 3 weeks. — [Arch2O](https://www.arch2o.com/?p=131762); [Architectuul](https://architectuul.com/architecture/bruder-klaus-field-chapel); [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/106352/bruder-klaus-field-chapel-peter-zumthor/); [NRW Skulptur](https://nrw-skulptur.net/en/skulptur/bruder-klaus-feldkapelle/)
- Commons dates it 2005–2007, describes it as a privately endowed chapel, and has a category of about 57 files. — [Commons category](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Feldkapelle_(Wachendorf))
- Material carrying the character: rammed concrete with burnt-out timber formwork, cast-glass plugs in the tie holes, and a lead-tin floor.
- One-liner: a building whose interior is the ash-print of the wood that made it.
- Official (not fetched): https://www.feldkapelle.de

The author field reads "jda" and the uploader is "Auhage", so check which name to credit. **[licence confirmed via search]** — [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bruder_Klaus_Feldkapelle.JPG)

**2. Therme Vals (now "7132 Thermal Baths")**: Peter Zumthor, completed 1996, Vals, Graubünden, Switzerland. Scene: exterior/interior, the material-as-architecture shot.
- Completed 1996. Faced with roughly 60,000 slabs of local Valser quartzite (the figure comes from the operator). Divisare calls the stone gneiss instead. The canton placed the building under a preservation order shortly after it opened. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7132_Thermal_Baths); [Illustrarch](https://illustrarch.com/articles/112251-peter-zumthor-therme-vals.html); [Divisare](https://divisare.com/projects/388269-peter-zumthor-morphosis-architects-thom-mayne-fabrice-fouillet-thermes-vals-at-7132-hotel)
- Material: thin quartzite courses laid as precise bands, so the wall is the structure and the finish at once.
- Official (not fetched): https://7132.com

**3. St Peter's Church (Sankt Petri kyrka), Klippan**: Sigurd Lewerentz, 1962–1966, Klippan, Skåne, Sweden. Scene: exterior and interior, the joint-as-character example.
- Lewerentz set one rule: never cut a brick. He supervised the placement of every brick for three years. Because only whole bricks were allowed, the mortar joints are thick and vary in size. The mortar was smeared over the brick faces rather than pointed flush. — [ArchEyes](https://archeyes.com/?p=37830); [DAC](https://dac.dk/en/knowledgebase/architecture/church-of-saint-peter); [Lynch Architects / AJ](https://www.lyncharchitects.com/news-reviews/lewerentz/); [Caruso, "Lewerentz and a Material Basis for Form" (PDF)](https://carusostjohn.com/media/files/Adam_Caruso_-_Sigurd_Lewerentz_and_a_Material_Basis_for_Form.pdf)
- Commons gives Helsingborg brick as the main material and 1966 as the completion date. The building is listed as RAÄ no. 21300000003735. — [Commons category](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Sankt_Petri_kyrka,_Klippan)
- One-liner: the irregular whole-brick rule becomes the ornament. This is the clearest "joint holds the material" building.

**4. Yakisugi House and Takasugi-an**: Terunobu Fujimori, Yakisugi House 2007 (Nagano City) and Takasugi-an 2004 (Chino, Nagano), Japan. Scene: house/exterior, the charred cedar example.
- Yakisugi House (also called Charred Cedar House) is clad in charred cedar. Boards over about 2 m normally warp during charring. Fujimori, the clients and friends charred about 400 boards of roughly 8 m each in a single day. The gaps left by warping were filled with plaster, which gives the facade its stripes. — [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2009/03/11/yakisugi-house-by-terunobu-fujimori/); [The Modern House](https://www.themodernhouse.com/journal/house-of-the-week-yakisugi-house-charred-cedar-house-by-terunobu-fujimori/)
- The Takasugi-an completion year (2004) is from memory and was not checked in this session.

  - [Category:Yakisugi](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Yakisugi) for generic charred-cedar textures.

**5. Lunuganga**: Geoffrey Bawa, 1948/49 to 2003, Bentota, Sri Lanka. Scene: house/exterior and living room (lived-in, slowly accreted).
- Bawa bought a former rubber estate (January 1948 in one account, 1949 in another) and reshaped it with no master plan until his death in 2003. The estate is preserved as he left it. It is run by the Lunuganga Trust as a boutique hotel, and the garden is open to visitors. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunuganga); [The Island](https://island.lk/a-visionary-garden-designed-with-such-care/)
- Official: geoffreybawa.com/lunuganga (listed in the Wikipedia infobox, not fetched).

By Labeet, own work, Aug 2012, CC BY-SA 3.0.
- Licence not confirmed: [exterior](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lunuganga,_Bentota,_Sri_Lanka..JPG) and [bungalow back](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lunuganga_-_Bungalow_Back.jpg).

**6. Ise Jingū (Naikū)**: rebuilt every 20 years. The latest Shikinen Sengū was 2013, and the next is planned for 2033. Ise, Mie, Japan. Scene: exterior, as a counterpoint where coherence comes from renewal of the craft rather than accumulated patina.
- Commons has Naikū photos under mixed licences: one CC0, a 2019 photo under CC BY-SA 3.0 + GFDL, others under CC BY-SA 4.0 and CC BY 3.0, and a 1983 aerial photo with attribution-only reuse from the Japanese land ministry. ### Inferences
- For a single "house/exterior" slide: **Klippan** best shows *precision holding irregularity* (strict rule, irregular result), **Bruder Klaus** best shows *material memory*, and the **Yakisugi House** best shows *charred cedar*. All three have photos with confirmed licences.
- **Ise** shows the opposite logic to patina (renewal instead of ageing) and may work better as a footnote than as a lead image.

### Gaps
- No Commons photos were found for **Zumthor's Haldenstein studio/house** or the **Leis houses (Oberhus/Unterhus, Vals)**. Commons lists Haldenstein only as his work location. Nearest substitute: [Werkraum Haus, Andelsbuch](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Werkraum_Haus_von_Peter_Zumthor_in_Andelsbuch_1.JPG) (licence not confirmed).
- No Commons results for **Studio Mumbai's Palmyra House**, **John Pawson's Home Farm** or the **Pawson House**. Use press photography (Dezeen/ArchDaily) with permission, or leave them out.
- No specific Commons file of a named building with green copper patina had its licence confirmed.

## Offices / workspaces: which real studios show coherence through materials rather than branding?

### Takeaway
The best-documented free photos are of the **Wharton Esherick Studio** (a public-domain HABS exterior plus CC BY interior details), the **George Nakashima workshop** (CC BY-SA), and **Scarpa's Olivetti showroom** (CC BY 2.0 stair photos). **Judd's 101 Spring Street** and **Marfa** are well documented in the press but have almost no free photos of the buildings themselves. The **Vitsoe 606** has a CC BY-SA photo uploaded by Vitsoe itself.

### Cited Findings

**1. Wharton Esherick House & Studio**: Wharton Esherick, 1926–1966, Malvern (Valley Forge Mountain), Pennsylvania. Scenes: office/workspace and house.
- Hand-built over 40 years. Designated a National Historic Landmark on 19 April 1993. The famous spiral staircase (1930) is made of hand-hewn red oak slabs, with the bolts on its cantilevered treads left exposed. It was shown at the 1939 New York World's Fair. The museum states the site is 12 acres. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wharton_Esherick_Museum); [National Trust / Saving Places](https://savingplaces.org/stories/free-spirit-home-and-studio-artist-wharton-esherick); [NHL nomination text](https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NHLS/73001615_text); [Museum site](https://whartonesherickmuseum.org/about/)
- Material/joint: sculpted oak and other hardwoods, with exposed bolts and pegs as visible fixings.
- **Commons photos [licences confirmed via search]:**
  - [Wharton Esherick House & Studio, 1520 Horseshoe Trail (HABS)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Wharton_Esherick_House_&_Studio,_1520_Horsehoe_Trail,_Malvern_(Chester_County,_Pennsylvania).jpg). Public domain, credit "Library of Congress, Prints & Photographs Division, HABS".
  - [Wharton Esherick workshop and homestead on Valley Forge Mountain](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Wharton_Esherick_workshop_and_homestead_on_Valley_Forge_Mountain.jpg). CC BY 2.0. The author is given as Ben Yanis (Flickr: BarnyardBBS).
  - [Fireplace, andirons and doorway by Esherick (1935–38), Philadelphia Museum of Art](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fireplace,_andirons,_and_doorway_by_Wharton_Esherick_(1935%E2%80%931938).jpg). CC BY 2.0, by Paul Lowry (Flickr).
  - [Category:Wharton Esherick House](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Wharton_Esherick_House)

**2. George Nakashima House, Studio and Workshop**: George Nakashima, 1946 onward (Conoid Studio 1957), Aquetong Rd, Solebury Twp / New Hope, Pennsylvania. Scene: office/workspace.
- Listed on the National Register in 2008 and as a National Historic Landmark on 23 April 2014. The Conoid Studio (1957) has an experimental reinforced-concrete shell roof. The workshop still makes furniture, run by his daughter Mira, and the showroom and studio are open on Saturdays. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Nakashima_House,_Studio_and_Workshop); [Archipedia (SAH)](https://archipediavm.upress.virginia.edu/buildings/PA-02-BU21); [Metropolis](https://metropolismag.com/programs/root-system/)
- Material/joint: free-edge black walnut slabs with butterfly (bow-tie) keys across the cracks. The butterfly-key detail is general knowledge and was not re-sourced here.
- Official (not fetched): https://nakashimawoodworkers.com

**3. Olivetti Showroom (Negozio Olivetti)**: Carlo Scarpa, 1957–58, Procuratie Vecchie, Piazza San Marco, Venice. Scene: office/workspace and showroom. The brand is present, but its coherence is carried by marble, teak, stucco and water.

Generali owns the property, and it has since been restored; the FAI-run reopening date is not covered by this search. — [Commons file description](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Carlo_scarpa,_architect-_olivetti_showroom_stairs,_venice_1957-58_(33777096671).jpg)

**4. Donald Judd: 101 Spring Street (New York) and Marfa**: building 1870 (Nicholas Whyte), bought by Judd 1968. Spring Street restored by Architecture Research Office and opened 2013. Scene: office/workspace and living.
- Judd gave each floor a single use (meeting, eating, working, socialising, sleeping) under his idea of "permanent installation". A 1970 Dan Flavin light work runs along the sleeping floor. Most sources put this on the 5th floor; Dezeen says the 4th. — [Places Journal](https://placesjournal.org/article/101-spring-street/); [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/?p=319810); [Historic Artists' Homes & Studios](https://artistshomes.org/sites/judd-foundation/)
- The 1870 date, the architect and the 2013 opening are general knowledge and were not re-sourced here.
- Material/joint: cast-iron facade, original pine floors, and Judd's own plywood and pine furniture with exposed butt joints. Official: https://juddfoundation.org (not fetched).
- **Commons photo (Marfa):** [Donald_Judd_Concrete_Blocks.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Donald_Judd_Concrete_Blocks.jpg), Chinati Foundation. The photo is CC BY-SA 3.0. The uploader claims the 1984 sculpture is US public domain because it was published without a copyright notice; that claim was not verified. **[photo licence confirmed via search]** — [Category:Chinati Foundation](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Chinati_Foundation)

**5. Vitsoe 606 Universal Shelving System**: Dieter Rams, 1960. Scene: office/workspace. The precise system that outlasts its contents.
- **Commons photo:** [606-Universal-Shelving-System.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:606-Universal-Shelving-System.jpg). CC BY-SA 3.0. Vitsoe is listed as uploader and copyright holder. **[licence confirmed via search]**
- Related: [Dieter Rams: Braun stereo and Vitsoe 620 armchair (4945811417)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dieter_Rams_--_Braun_Stereo_System_&_Vitsoe_Armchair_620_(4945811417).jpg). **[licence NOT confirmed]**
- Official (not fetched): https://www.vitsoe.com

### Inferences
- For the "office/workspace" slide, **Esherick's studio** (a free HABS image plus a CC BY interior) and **Nakashima's workshop** (CC BY-SA) are the safest choices for free photos and show craft coherence most clearly.
- **Scarpa's Olivetti showroom** is a useful nuance: a company space whose identity is carried by materials and joints, not the logo.
- **Judd** fits the argument best, but has few free images of the buildings.

### Gaps
- No free photo of Rams's own house in Kronberg was found.
- No free Jean Prouvé office pieces (e.g. Bureau Présidence, Compas desk) turned up in search.
- The Olivetti showroom's restoration and reopening status after Generali's ownership is not covered by this search.

## Living rooms / couches: which real sofas and furniture embody patina, and what is the counter-case?

### Takeaway
The leather/oak patina pieces (**Mogensen's Spanish Chair** and **2213 sofa**) and the joint-led modular sofas (**De Sede DS-600**, hinge plus zipper; **Camaleonda**, cable, hook and ring) are well documented, but Commons has only thin coverage of them. **Kettle's Yard** is the strongest documented counter-case (a house kept "by and large as Jim and Helen left it"), and **Lunuganga** is a parallel case.

### Cited Findings

**1. Børge Mogensen: Spanish Chair (1958) and Sofa 2213 (1962)**, Fredericia, Denmark. Scene: living room.
- Spanish Chair: solid oak frame with a seat and back of thick vegetable-tanned saddle leather held by buckles. It was launched in 1958. One edition is listed as made in 1967. — [Miliashop product listing](https://miliashop.com/en/chair/19086-the-spanish-fredericia-chair.html)
- Sofa 2213 was designed in 1962 as part of the 22 series. It has cognac or patinated leather on oak legs. — [Bilanz](https://www.bilanz.ch/enjoy/manufaktur-fredericia-renaissance-des-daenischen-designs/d97gen1)
- Material: vegetable-tanned leather that darkens with use, on oak with visible joints.
- Official (not fetched): https://www.fredericia.com
- **Commons:** "Børge Mogensen - Spannish chair.jpg" (spelled that way, 4000×3000), in the Designmuseum Danmark collection category. URL as derived: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:B%C3%B8rge_Mogensen_-_Spannish_chair.jpg. **[licence NOT confirmed. Check whether the filename resolves.]**
- Related, confirmed: [J52 (1944)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:B%C3%B8rge_Mogensen_-_J52_(1944).jpg) CC BY-SA 4.0, and [FDB Møbler chair (Nasjonalmuseet)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chair_by_B%C3%B8rge_Mogensen_for_FDB_M%C3%B8bler.jpg) CC BY-SA 4.0. — [Category:Børge Mogensen](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:B%C3%B8rge_Mogensen)

**2. De Sede DS-600 ("Non-Stop" / "Tatzelwurm")**: 1972, De Sede, Klingnau, Switzerland. Design credits vary: Ueli Berger, Eleonore Peduzzi-Riva, Heinz Ulrich and Klaus Vogt. Scene: living room.
- Each element is an L-shaped hard foam core with a leather cushion. The elements join with a plug-in hinge and a hide-and-zipper construction, so the joint is literally the design. De Sede reportedly says it never used the "Non Stop" name. — [Design-Market listing](https://www.design-market.us/240019-vintage-ds-600-non-stop-modular-sofa-in-fullblack-leather-by-de-sede-1972.html); [1stDibs](https://www.1stdibs.com/furniture/seating/sectional-sofas/de-sede-ds-600-non-stop-shape-modular-sofa-black-leather/id-f_48447882/). These are dealer sources only.
- Official (not fetched): https://www.desede.ch
- **Commons:** none found.

**3. Camaleonda**: Mario Bellini, 1970, C&B Italia / B&B Italia, Italy. Shown at MoMA in 1972 (*Italy: The New Domestic Landscape*), produced until 1979, reissued in 2020. Scene: living room.
- The capitonné surface comes from Bellini's 1970 system of cables, hooks and rings, which also lets the 90×90 cm modules be unhooked and recombined. The 2020 reissue kept the original cutting pattern and rebuilt the interior from recyclable, separable layers. — [Space Furniture](https://www.spacefurniture.com.au/products/b-b-italia-camaleonda-sofa); [Galerie](https://galeriemagazine.com/bb-italia-mario-bellini-camaleonda-sofa/); [Lifestyle Asia](https://lifestyleasia-onemega.com/living/camaleonda-returns-reissue-after-50-years-mario-bellini-bb-italia/)
- Official (not fetched): https://www.bebitalia.com
- **Commons:** a file "Camaleonda-modular-sofa 10068.jpg" appears in [Category:Mario Bellini](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Mario_Bellini). URL as derived: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Camaleonda-modular-sofa_10068.jpg. **[licence and author NOT confirmed]**

**4. Soriana**: Afra & Tobia Scarpa, 1969, Cassina, Italy. Scene: living room.
- Polyurethane foam is gathered into a "bundle" by chrome-plated steel clamps, so the clamp is the joint. **6. Kettle's Yard (counter-case)**: Jim & Helen Ede. Converted cottages lived in 1958–1973, extension 1970 by Leslie Martin and David Owers, reopened February 2018 after a refurbishment by Jamie Fobert. Castle Street, Cambridge. Scene: living room, as the counter-case.
- Ede gave the house and collection to the University of Cambridge in 1966 and stayed on as honorary curator until 1973. The house is "by and large as Jim and Helen left it". It reopened on 10 February 2018 after two years of closure. Ede arranged art, furniture, glass, ceramics and pebbles as one "harmonic whole", with the exact placement of each pebble treated as meaningful. — [Kettle's Yard history](https://www.kettlesyard.cam.ac.uk/history-of-kettles-yard/); [The Art Newspaper](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2018/02/06/kettles-yardwhere-modern-art-meets-pot-plants-and-pebblesreopens-after-two-year-facelift); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kettle%27s_Yard); [Flickr page quoting Ede (1984)](https://www.flickr.com/photos/hedgiecc/3465770745)
- The Fobert attribution is from memory.
- Why it is the counter-case: a Gesamtkunstwerk frozen by bequest. Its coherence is curatorial and protected from further life, which is the totalising risk.
