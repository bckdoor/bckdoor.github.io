## 1. Car interiors without logo display: character from material and age

### Takeaway
The best-documented logo-free car examples are (a) Singer Vehicle Design's reimagined 964-based Porsche 911s, which use woven leather, brass grommets and wood or cork trim, and (b) the preservation movement at concours events: Pebble Beach preservation classes and the FIVA Preservation trophy, whose judges would rather see an original seat repaired than replaced. Good Commons photos exist for a Singer exterior, a Porsche 356 dashboard and a Citroën DS interior. I found no Commons photo of a Singer interior or a Land Rover Series cabin.

### Cited Findings

**Singer Vehicle Design: Porsche 911 "Reimagined" (Los Angeles; built from 1989–94 964-generation cars, 2009–present) · CAR**
- Material character: tan or black leather seats with Singer's "famed woven leather inserts" and brass grommets. On some cars the matching woven leather extends to door panels, rear seats and dashboard — [stuttcars sale listing](https://stuttcars.com/?p=479719); [stuttcars C4 listing](https://www.stuttcars.com/for-sale-1990-porsche-911-carrera-4-coupe-by-singer/)

**Bugatti Veyron Fbg par Hermès (2008) · CAR (counter-example / brand collab)**
- Shown at the 78th Geneva Motor Show, March 2008. Inner surfaces were "designed and sheathed in bull calfskin by the Hermès workshops in Paris". Colours were ebony/étoupe or ebony/brick; price €1.55 million before tax — [Bugatti newsroom](https://newsroom.bugatti.com/press-releases/the-bugatti-veyron-fbg-par-herm-s-)
- Correction to the brief: the "saddle-stitch" reference is on the outside. The air vents around the wheel rims reproduce the look of Hermès saddle-stitching, and the centre wheel locks carry an "H" — [Bugatti newsroom](https://newsroom.bugatti.com/press-releases/the-bugatti-veyron-fbg-par-herm-s-). So the car combines craft with logo display ("H" locks, the name itself). It works better as a contrast or bridge example than as a pure "craft way" example.

**Patina and preservation culture at concours events · CAR (context)**
- Hagerty on Pebble Beach preservation judging: FIVA sets its own criteria for the preservation awards, focused on the best-preserved original cars with condition and provenance that support their originality. Judges reward original craftsmanship and finishes and would rather see a seat or door panel repaired than replaced — [Hagerty](https://hagerty.com/media/?p=3246)
- Fortune (2015) linked Pebble Beach's preservation classes to a growing enthusiast preference for original condition. A 1964 Ferrari 250 GTL won the Ferrari Preservation class that year, with an unrestored Superamerica second — [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2015/08/14/ferrari-preservation)
- 2016: a 1902 Thomas won the FIVA Preservation Trophy, and there were separate prewar and postwar preservation classes — [zwischengas](https://www.zwischengas.com/de/VC/veranstaltungsberichte/Pebble-Beach-Concours-d-Elegance-2016.html)
- One magazine search result claims an unrestored 1934 Bugatti Type 59 was the first Preservation Class car to win Best of Show at Pebble Beach, in 2024. **Unverified**: single source, not checked against official results — [search result: magnetomagazine](https://www.magnetomagazine.com/page/3/?s=Figoni)

### Inferences
- Singer works as a "joint and material" image: woven leather and brass grommets carry the identity in place of a badge. The strongest freely licensed Singer images are exteriors, though, so an interior slide would need a press photo or a link.
- Commons' DS and 356 interiors show material and instrument character rather than documented patina. For a "patina car" slide, the stronger move may be to cite the FIVA and Pebble Beach preservation rule (repair rather than replace) as the cultural principle.

### Gaps
- No Commons photo found of a Singer interior, a documented survivor-condition cabin, Volvo/Saab wool upholstery, or Rolls-Royce/Bentley wood veneer work. The Rolls-Royce/Bentley and coachbuilt-interior questions were not researched for lack of time.
- Commons file pages could not be opened directly, so authors and licenses for several category-level files remain unchecked.

## 2. Clothes: getting dressed

### Takeaway
Boro and sashiko have an excellent museum photo on Commons (CC BY 4.0, Swedish National Museums of World Culture). Raw selvedge denim fades have a CC BY-SA 4.0 Commons photo. Savile Row has a 1944 Ministry of Information photo of the Henry Poole workroom. On the repair-service side, Barbour's re-wax and repair service and Patagonia's Reno repair facility are well documented.

### Cited Findings

**Boro textile with sashiko (Japan; mostly 19th to early 20th century rural textiles) · DRESS**
- [File:Boro textile from Japan, with sashiko.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Boro_textile_from_Japan,_with_sashiko.jpg). Back of an indigo-dyed boro quilt; the sashiko is both functional and decorative. Photographed 2020 by Karl Zetterström, Statens museer för världskultur (Swedish National Museums of World Culture). CC BY 4.0. Used on the en/sv/fi/cs Wikipedia boro articles — [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Boro_textile_from_Japan,_with_sashiko.jpg)
- Commons defines boro as Japanese textiles that have been mended or patched together. Sashiko is running-stitch embroidery first used to reinforce worn or thin layers so cloth could be reused, and is now often ornamental — [Category:Boro (textile)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Boro_(textile)); [Category:Sashiko](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Sashiko)
- **Do not use** "File:Boro shirts 1994-2010.jpg": it shows Middlesbrough F.C. ("Boro") football shirts, not boro textile — [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Boro_shirts_1994-2010.jpg)
- Amuse Museum (Asakusa, Tokyo) showed folklorist Chūzaburō Tanaka's boro collection and **closed on 31 March 2019**. A touring BORO exhibition was planned for 2019–21, but I found no confirmation that a new museum opened. Reported collection size: about 30,000 items, kept in Aomori — [Japan Travel (archived)](https://es.jal.japantravel.com/tokyo/amuse-museum/24436); [Wikipedia: Amuse Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amuse_Museum)

**Raw selvedge denim fades (Full Count, Japan; fades are personal and built up over years) · DRESS**

**Savile Row bespoke: Henry Poole & Co. (founded 1806) · DRESS**
- [File:Savile Row- Tailoring at Henry Poole and Co., London, England, UK, 1944 D21864.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Savile_Row-_Tailoring_at_Henry_Poole_and_Co.,_London,_England,_UK,_1944_D21864.jpg). - Official site: https://henrypoole.com (from general knowledge, not fetched). Anderson & Sheppard was not researched.

**Barbour waxed jacket re-wax and repair (UK; service ongoing) · DRESS**
- Customers can send a jacket back at any time to be re-waxed, cleaned, repaired or altered. Barbour recommends re-waxing roughly once a year depending on wear. The full service covers waxed-cotton garments only, and some items are judged beyond repair. The Duke Street store in Mayfair has the brand's first in-store re-waxing station — [Barbour Re-Wax and Repair](https://www.barbour.com/gb/repair-rewax.html)
- Note: Barbour jackets carry a visible logo badge, so this is a repair-culture example rather than a logo-free one.

**Patagonia Worn Wear repairs (Reno, NV) · DRESS**
- Complex repairs go to Patagonia's Reno facility, which Patagonia calls one of the largest apparel repair operations in the US. Typical turnaround is 8–10 weeks, around 12 at peak (help page updated June 2026) — [Patagonia Bend help](https://help.patagoniabend.com/en/articles/1780698490-warranty-repairs)
- Scale figures conflict: about 45 technicians and about 40,000 repairs a year (Berkeley-Haas, 2017) against about 75 repair staff (later Snowboarder's Journal visit) — [Berkeley-Haas](https://haas.berkeley.edu/responsible-business/blog/posts/patagonias-worn-wear-2017/); [Snowboarder's Journal](https://thesnowboardersjournal.com/blogs/exclusives/patagonia-repairs)

### Inferences
- The boro photo is the strongest single "repair as coherence" image in this whole set: museum provenance, a clean CC BY 4.0 license, and a visible repair joint (the sashiko).
- The denim fade image shows patina written by one wearer's body, which suits a "no logo, but unmistakably mine" argument.

### Gaps
- Not researched: Margiela Artisanal, Lemaire, The Row, Jil Sander (logo-free), Anderson & Sheppard, and the visible mending movement (e.g. Tom of Holland). No Commons photos of these were sought.
- No Commons photo of a Patagonia repair or a Barbour re-wax was found.

## 3. Shoes: Goodyear welt, resoling, lasts kept for clients

### Takeaway
Goodyear-welted construction is made to be resoled. Crockett & Jones run a factory repair service in Northampton that rebuilds shoes on their original last. John Lobb keeps customers' wooden lasts in St James's, about 20,000–30,000 of them, so later pairs match the first. Commons has Goodyear welt diagrams, but I found no photo of a resoled shoe or of a Lobb last.

### Cited Findings
- **Goodyear welt construction.** A welt strip is stitched to the upper and insole, and the outsole is stitched to the welt. Because of this, the sole can be replaced several times — [US patent 8336229 (summary)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8336229)
- Commons: [File:Goodyear welt.png](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Goodyear_welt.png) (cross-section diagram) and [File:Example of a fake Goodyear welt.png](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Example_of_a_fake_Goodyear_welt.png). Licenses and authors not returned (verify).
- **Crockett & Jones (Northampton, est. 1879) · SHOES.** All C&J shoes are fully repairable and can be sent back to the factory through the shops. Repairs are done on the original last, and the company says customers get back what amounts to "a new pair of shoes" — [The Gentleman's Journal](https://www.thegentlemansjournal.com//article/heres-how-crockett-jones-give-your-shoes-a-new-lease-of-life/); the Royal Exchange (London) shop offers repairs, refurbishment and re-soling — [Royal Exchange](https://www.theroyalexchange.co.uk/crockett-and-jones/). Official site: https://www.crockettandjones.com (not fetched). No official price or turnaround found.
- **John Lobb Ltd (9 St James's Street, London; bespoke) · SHOES.** Lasts are kept for future orders, re-adjusted when a customer's feet change, numbered and stored on shelves. The collection is estimated at 20,000–30,000 lasts, and Churchill's lasts are in the archive below the shop. Accounts of the storage conflict: one visitor describes racks of lasts "in no apparent order" — [The Manufacturer](https://www.themanufacturer.com/articles/john-lobb-the-last-shall-be-first/); [VoiceMap St James's tour](https://voicemap.me/tour/london/the-finest-street-in-london-a-stroll-around-st-james-s/sites/john-lobb-3); [Wikipedia: John Lobb Bootmaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lobb_Bootmaker)
- Commons has a [Category:Cobblers](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Cobblers) (repair shops, including a shoe-sole sewing image) and a "Shoe Last.jpg" in Category:Shoemaking in Nigeria — [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Shoemakers)

### Inferences
- The stored last is a strong metaphor for "coherence without a logo": the person, not the brand mark, is the template that is kept. Note that John Lobb Ltd (London, bespoke) is a separate company from Hermès-owned John Lobb (Paris, ready-to-wear); this comes from general knowledge, not from the sources above.

### Gaps
- Edward Green was not researched. No freely licensed photo of a resoled welted shoe or of the Lobb last archive was found. Licenses for the Goodyear diagrams were not checked.

## 4. Bags: saddle-stitched, vegetable-tanned leather, patina

### Takeaway
Frank Clegg (Fall River, MA) is a well-documented logo-light maker of harness/bridle-leather bags that develop patina, and Permanent Style has a "five years later" patina review. Commons has many Hermès Birkin photos with clear licenses, but they show the status-object side more than the patina side.

- **Frank Clegg Leatherworks (Fall River, Massachusetts) · BAG.** Self-taught maker working in an old textile mill, with his son Ian in the business. ## 5. Tableware and objects: morning tea

### Takeaway
Kaikado (Kyoto, since 1875) is the ideal patina object: double-walled copper, brass or tin caddies that darken with handling, and V&A-collected. No Commons photo of one was found. Commons has good kintsugi, Nanbu tetsubin, Hagi ware and Opinel images. For Leica M black-paint brassing, the practice is documented but there is no confirmed Commons photo of a brassed body.

### Cited Findings

**Kaikado chazutsu tea caddy (Kyoto, est. 1875; six generations) · TEA**
- Hand-made, double-walled caddies with a tinplate interior and a copper, brass or tin exterior; the lid seals tightly. The designs are largely unchanged since 1875, and the metal develops a patina with regular use — [Wikipedia: Kaikado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaikado); [Pamono story](https://www.pamono.de/stories/steeped-in-history)
- A retailer's rough timeline for visible colour change: copper 2–3 months, brass 1–2 years, tin 3–5 years. This is retailer guidance, not Kaikado's — [Yoshien](https://www.yoshien.com/en/tea-caddy-kaikado-brass-100g.html)
- The V&A reportedly acquired four Kaikado caddies (reported, not verified with the V&A) — [Wikipedia: Kaikado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaikado). Sixth-generation maker Takahiro Yagi features in Homo Faber 2022 — [Homo Faber](https://2022.homofaber.com/en/discover/discover-takahiro-yagi). Official site: https://www.kaikado.jp (not fetched).
- No Commons file found. The closest is [Category:Tea boxes](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Tea_boxes).

**Hagi ware (Hagi, Yamaguchi Prefecture; since 1604) · TEA**
- Commons lists Hagi ware as a traditional Japanese craft with an inception date of 1604 — [Category:Hagi ware](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Hagi_ware)
- The file "Hagi ware Japanese tea bowl, 18th-19th century, Freer Gallery of Art.jpg" (3,547 × 2,661) is in [Category:Chawan](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Chawan). License not returned; Freer/Smithsonian Open Access images are often CC0, but **verify**. Hagi ware is known for *nanabake*, "seven changes": the glaze crazing absorbs tea and the bowl's colour changes with use. That point comes from general knowledge and was not sourced here.

**Leica M black paint and brassing (M3 from 1954; MP 2003; M10-R Black Paint 2022) · object**
- Black-paint Leicas wear through to the brass underneath. Many collectors like this and do not treat it as a defect, and genuine factory black paint carries a premium. Repaints are common and hard to tell from originals — [Macfilos 2017](https://www.macfilos.com/2017/04/25/2017-4-25-black-paint-leicas-much-ado-about-nothing/)
- Forum anecdote (not audited): heavily brassed M3s were fetching more than cleaner examples — [l-camera-forum](https://www.l-camera-forum.com/topic/329803-educate-me-about-the-variations-of-leica-m3-black-paint-original)
- Leica marketed the M10-R Black Paint as designed to wear over time and reveal the brass — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/stunning-leica-m-10r-black-paint-edition-is-designed-to-age-gracefully)
- Commons: [File:Leica MP (2003) and Leica M3 (1954).jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Leica_MP_(2003)_and_Leica_M3_(1954).jpg) shows a black-paint MP beside a 1954 M3. Whether brassing is visible, and the license, were not confirmed (verify).
- Caveat: Leica bodies carry the red dot and engraved script logo.
